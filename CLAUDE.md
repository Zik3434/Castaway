# Castaway

A pixel-art fishing, hunting and mining game. You are a fisherman stranded on an island. You fish your way off it, then fish, hunt and dig your way across a fictional sea of wildly different islands, buying rods, lines, bait, weapons, picks, lamps and boats, and (around 200 hours in) a starship, to fish in space. Travel is by chart; on each island you walk a short strip between the fishing spot, the village and the hunting ground.

`index.html` is the entire playable prototype (vanilla JS, canvas, no build step). It is published as a website at https://zik3434.github.io/Castaway/ (GitHub Pages, deployed by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` from the repository root on every push to `main`); open it there or open the file in a browser. Read this file before changing anything; it holds every design decision made so far, what is placeholder, and what comes next.

## Decisions already made (do not relitigate without asking)

- **Stranded is the hook, and it is used.** You start on an island with no market, with one rod and one line (a second line costs $60 at the first port; `SLOT_PRICES`). Fish go in a bucket. Driftwood comes up on the lines; six pieces build a raft. The raft is the first boat. The first port buys the whole haul, and that is the moment money starts to exist. Selling is irrelevant until you leave the island.
- **Gear is four ladders, and each does one thing.** The **rod** widens the net in the fight and carries `luck`, which tilts every rod cast toward rarer fish (weight × (1 + rank × luck × 0.5), rank 0 common to 3 epic, 1 for a legend); five rods from the stick to the star-iron. **Bait** gates species by tier and adds luck of its own (scraps 0, shrimp 0.3, squid 0.8). The **line** is a test weight (`LINE_TEST`: 10 lb at the start, then 20, 40, 80, 150, 300, 600 and a 2,000 lb cable): a species whose smallest adult outweighs the line never bites, and anything else is held to the line's weight (the catch card says so, with a LINE HELD pill; large and gigantic variants are lost when held). Legends need the line that takes their smallest, then run free. A cast with nothing in the pool is refused with a toast naming the line. **A fish too big for the line breaks off, and the game says so every time** (asked for so that a player who needs a heavier line can see it): a fish rolled heavier than the line is no longer always held to it: it breaks off with odds `T.snapHeld` (0.35, + 0.2 for every line-weight it is over, at most 0.8) and is held (LINE HELD) otherwise, and a species too heavy for the line at its smallest, which still never lands, takes the bait and breaks off with odds `T.snapShare` × its share of the water (at most `snapMax` 0.3; `POOL_LOST` is what the last `pool()` left out for the line). `rollCatch` returns `{ snap: true }` and at the moment the bite would have come `snapLine` toasts *Line 2: SNAP. Something too heavy for your 10 lb line took it and broke off.* (from the third on this line it adds the count and *You need a heavier line*), and the count stays on the panel in red under the rig note (`S.broke = { line, n }`, `brokeNote`, reset by a new line). The bait is spent. Sim over the first eight islands, rod: on 10 lb line 1.6% of casts break off on scraps, 17.5% on shrimp, 24% on squid; on 20 lb 0.2 / 10 / 14%; on 40 lb 0.1 / 4 / 6%; on 80 lb 0 / 2 / 4%. Never in the prologue, never a legend. The **bucket** is bought in sizes (`BUCKETS`: pail 10, basket 20, barrel 35, ice chest 60, hold 100); `bucketCap()` replaces the old constant. **Everything bought is kept**: `rodsOwned`, `linesOwned`, `pailsOwned` list every rung owned, `rod`/`line`/`pail` are the ones in use, the rig sheet swaps between them, and the shop sold the rung above the best owned until gear became island-made (the next bullet). Saves are `v: 11`; a v9 save gets the line its rod implies and a basket, and v10 saves own every rung below what they hold, so nobody loses what they could already catch or carry.
- **Gear is made on the islands: every shop's pieces are its own, and the shop says whether one is an upgrade** (asked for with a photo of a shop selling the same Star-iron rod, 150 lb line and fish basket as every other port: "make islands have shop unique items, but it makes it clear if an item is an upgrade from your current items"). The ladders (`RODS`, `LINE_TEST`, `BUCKETS`, `WEAPONS`, `CLOAKS`, `BOOTS`, `PACKS`, `PICKS`, `LAMPS`, `SACKS`) are **grades** now: what a thing can be made in, each with the `tier` of island that makes it (rods, lines and buckets were given tiers: cane 1, carbon 2, steel 3, star-iron 5; 20 lb 1, 40 lb 2, 80 lb 3, 150 lb 4, 300 lb 5, 600 lb and the cable 6; basket 1, barrel 2, chest 3, hold 5). A shop no longer sells the rung above the best you own. It sells **one piece of each kind it stocks** (rod, line, weapon and pick always; bucket, cloak, boots, game bag, lamp and sack where its shelves keep them: `shopKinds`), **made on its island and sold nowhere else** (`madeHere`): in a grade that goes with the island's tier (`madeGrade`: its tier's own, one of them where a tier has several; at `T_MADE.grade` odds the grade below, 22%, or the one above, 18%), with **numbers of its own** (`madeAt`, `T_MADE`): a rod leans to the net (a point or two more of the track, luck ×0.72 to 0.9) or to luck (×1.15 to 1.4, about a point of net less) or neither; a line holds 0.8 to 1.3 times its grade's weight (a *Tin Row 195 lb line*); a bucket, bag or sack has 0.85 to 1.3 times the room; a weapon shoots true (band ×1.06 to 1.14, power ×0.8 to 0.95) or hits heavy (the reverse); a cloak is a little calmer or less; a lamp carries 0.9 to 1.25 times the oil; a third of picks are tempered half a point harder (hardness is not always whole now: 6.5 pries a 6 and not a 7); boots differ only in price. It is named for its island (*Naruto Gate steel rod*; `madeBy`) and priced by how good a piece of its grade it is (`T_MADE.price`, 0.8 to 1.35 of the grade's price), and everything about it is a fixed hash of the island and the kind (`madeRnd`), so a port's pieces never change. 239 shops sell 1,745 pieces between them (16 shops at tier 1 to 73 at tier 6), and none is stored anywhere: a piece is worked out from its id. **An owned piece is kept as `grade@island`** (`steel@whirlpool`) in the same lists as before (`rodsOwned` and the rest) and resolved by `gearGet` (cached; `gearUsed(kind)` is the piece in use, and `rodOf`, `lineOf`, `bucketOf`, `WEAPON`, `CLOAK`, `BOOT`, `PACK`, `PICK`, `LAMP` and `SACK` all go through it). A plain grade id is what you start with and what an old save holds, so the save's shape did not change and `v` stays 17: an old save keeps every rung it owned, and they measure against the island-made pieces like any other. An icon is its grade's (`iconC` drops the `@island`), and so is the weapon in the hunter's hands. The top grade never falls below its ladder's own numbers on what it gates (the star-iron bow still takes everything; the cable still holds everything). **The shop says how each piece measures against yours** (`gearVerdict`, `gearCmp`, `gearSub`, `GEAR_SAY`, `GEAR_BRIEF`, `GEAR_TAG`): a tag by its name, **UPGRADE** (filled green: better than what you use in some way and worse in none), **TRADE-OFF** (brass: better one way, worse another: a net rod against a luck rod of the same grade), **NO BETTER** (grey: something you own is at least as good in every way, and it says which if that is not the one in use), **OWNED** or **IN USE**; a line saying which of yours it was held up to; every stat on a line of its own with ▲ in green, ▼ in red or = and yours beside it (*Net 42% of the track ▲ yours 40%*, *Luck 1.15 ▼ yours 1.4*); a bar of the tag's colour down the row; and the head of the shop counts them (*6 of them would better what you carry*). Buying puts a piece in use unless you own better (then it goes to your kit and the toast says so); a piece you own reads YOURS and is not sold twice. The rig and kit sheets list every piece owned, weakest first, with where it was made (`gearRowsOf`). **The chart remembers which ports hold an upgrade for you**: a visited shop's row carries *▲ 3 upgrades for you* (`shopUpgrades`), worked out against what you carry now, so you never have to remember where you saw the better line. The panel, the cards and the toasts use the short name (`gearShort`: *Steel rod · 90 lb line*). **The cable takes anything** (`all: true` on its grade, read by `pool`): the check that every shop's pieces are sound found two legends, The Thousand-year Fruit (1,572 kg at its smallest) and The Compass (1,180 kg), that outweighed even the 2,000 lb cable's 907 kg and so could never have bitten. What a tier can reach is about what it was (`snip_reach.js`: with the best line sold up to its tier, 190 of 195 ordinary tier-1 fish can bite, 270 of 275 at tier 2, all of them from tier 5; a tier's legends still mostly want a line from further out, 2 of 35 at tier 1 and 31 of 45 at tier 4; every fabled creature and relic of a tier is within reach of the best weapon and pick sold by that tier, the relics because a few ports of each tier make the pick a grade up). Not done, and worth doing if the pieces should differ in more than numbers: a quirk of its own for a port's piece (a rod that bites sooner at dusk, a lamp that shows gas). Checked by `snip_gear.js` run with `evtext.js` (every shop and kind makes a sound piece with its own id; the grades and the ranges by tier; the top grades against the heaviest fish, the heaviest game and the hardest stone) and `geartest.js` (at four shops of four tiers it buys every piece from the starting gear, each an upgrade, each put in use; opens every sheet; runs a stalk and a descent with them; and loads an old save, which keeps its ladder gear); `shopshot.js tag island WxH` shows a shop with a save like the photo's, buys a piece and carries the save to a fresh page (a `file://` page loses its storage on reload, so a reload is no test of a save).
- **Bigger bait, bigger fish, and a better price.** Every bait has a `size` beside its tier and luck (scraps 0, shrimp 0.6, squid 1.4; doubled from 0.3 and 0.7 when the first values were not felt) and `rollCatch` skews the size roll by it (f → f^(1/(1+size)), before the set line's quick-hook cap and the soak bonus), so on squid a middling fish lands three quarters of the way up its range and about one rod cast in three is trophy size, against one in seven on scraps and one in four on shrimp (sim over 200,000 rolls: 14.9%, 22.9%, 32.3%; a quick set-line hook sits at a median of 0.31, 0.48 and 0.61 of the range, still under its 0.8 cap). Every bait also has a `worth` (scraps 1, shrimp 1.25, squid 1.6) that multiplies what a fish taken on it sells for, rolled into the catch's value at cast time on top of the weight the size already adds, so a rod cast on shrimp is worth about 1.5× one on scraps and on squid about 2.2× (the set line much the same); the card's BAIT tile shows the multiplier (*Squid · ×1.6*), the shop's bait lines say what takes it runs bigger and sells for more, and the rig sheet's bait note says so without numbers. Driftwood and junk take no multiplier. **Better bait is also a different pool**: every bait has a `shun` (shrimp 0.35, squid 0.2) that a scrap-bait fish's weight drops to on it, while a fish whose `minBait` is the bait's tier keeps its ×1.5 and a fish between keeps its weight, and the bait's luck is 0.5 and 1.2 (from 0.3 and 0.8), so shrimp and squid pull the pool toward the fish that need them instead of adding them to a pool the scrap-takers still own (sim over the first eight islands' first spots on 40 lb line, rod: mean worth per catch $63 on scraps, $178 on shrimp, $275 on squid, from $62, $93 and $100 before; a quarter of squid catches are fish that take only bait, from 7%; mean difficulty 0.28 → 0.34; the set line moves the same way). That still landed $3 sticklebacks on squid from a trophy line, so the third pass replaced `shun` with two rules that make paid bait a different pool (asked for twice: "fish caught with bait that isn't free are usually much different than fish caught with scraps, and are much more profitable"): every bait has a `mouth` (shrimp 30 g, squid 250 g), the smallest a species' largest adult can be and still get the bait in its mouth, so gobies and sticklebacks never take a squid; and a `share` (shrimp 0.5, squid 0.25), the most the scrap-takers together can weigh against the fish that need the bait, so on squid the pool is mostly squid fish. Where a spot has nothing that needs the bait the scrap-takers that can take it keep their weights, and where nothing there could get it in its mouth the biggest fish present still take it, so no cast is refused for the bait and no pool empties (checked: 222,592 pools). The **trophy line is for big fish** too: a species whose largest adult is under `T.trophyMinKg` (300 g) never takes it. Sim on the first eight islands' first spots, 40 lb line: rod mean worth per catch $62 on scraps, $290 on shrimp, $488 on squid, and catches under $10 fall from 44% to 18% and 1%; the trophy line on squid $584 a catch with under 1% cheap; 58% of squid catches are fish that take only bait.
- **The sky is a bite gate.** Six skies (`WEATHER`: clear, overcast, rain, fog, storm, snow). Every island rolls its own every `T.weatherMs` (four game hours, so six changes a game day) from a fixed hash of the slot and the island id (`weatherAt`, `hash32`), so it never flickers, neighbouring islands differ, and a reload keeps it with nothing saved; the odds come from the island's climate (`CLIMATE`, chosen by `climateOf` from its scene: `mild` for most, `wet` where the island's own particles are rain, `cold` where they are snow or the shore is ice, and a fixed sky stays fixed: the thunderhead's and the standing bolt's `storm` is always storm, a `cloud` island is overcast, rain or fog, the ash islands clear, grey or storm, a `cave` island and the prologue sea always clear). A species can carry `weather: ['storm']`, and then it bites only under that sky, or `likes: { rain: 2.5 }`, which multiplies its weight under it; `pool` applies both to residents and strays, legends keep their hour and ignore the sky. 233 species are sky-only (183 storm, 49 fog, 1 snow: about one per island, the island's rarest single-home fish, preferring a rare one at a deep spot for the storm and a close one for the fog) and 437 like a sky (rain at river, lake and marsh spots, fog at piers and harbours, grey over deep water and shores, storm over deep water, snow on the cold islands), assigned by a scratchpad script that only gates a fish when every set-line pool it sits in keeps another windowless scrap-bait fish, so no pool empties under any sky (the headless check now runs every spot × hour × bait × rig × every sky the island's climate gives: 222,592 pools, none empty, and every sky-only fish has a home whose climate brings its sky). The HUD clock line ends with the sky (*12:40 · Day · Rain*), the tide line names it when it is not clear and rotates in a sky line when something that minds this sky is in the pool, the index says *storm only* or *best in rain* on a species (known or not), the catch card's third tile is SKY (*Dusk, rain*) and a sky-only catch carries a STORM FISH pill. The scene shows it (`drawWeatherFx`, after `sceneFx` on the shore, the strip, the hunt and both sailing scenes, never over an island's fixed sky): a cloud base a third of the way down to the horizon in grey, dark grey or near-black (a painted deck with ranks of cloud going away under it since the fourteenth pass: see that section), rain or snow over the water (an island whose own particles are rain or snow now shows them only under that sky, `sceneFx`), fog banks drifting at the horizon over a pale wash, and the storm's lightning. **Skies change gradually**: for the first game hour of a new slot (`T.weatherFadeMs`) the last sky fades out under the new one, `drawWeatherFx` drawing both through `drawWx` with complementary alphas, so rain thins into fog instead of switching on a frame; the HUD's word still turns at the slot. Seasons are still open.
- **Passive lines soak; they do not count down.** A set or trophy line has a small chance of a hook every second, so the wait is exponential and memoryless (`waitFor` samples it once at cast time from `T.setMean` / `T.trophyMean`, capped at `soakCap` means, so it still survives closing the game). The card shows time soaked, never time left. **The longer it soaks, the bigger the fish**: `soakBonus` is 0 at one mean and 1 at `soakGrow` means, and the size fraction climbs toward the top of the range with it, so a set line that sat a long while can land trophy size (about one hook in ten) while a quick hook is a small fish.
- **Passive lines bring volume and cash. Skill lines bring trophies.** A set line hooks common fish with no fight and only reaches trophy size after a long soak. The rod gives a bite you must answer within a few seconds, then a bar-and-fish fight; rare species come only from the rod or the trophy line.
- **The trophy line is the expensive passive line.** Long wait, needs better bait, hooks big fish, but you still have to land it in the fight when you come back. It exists so patience pays without replacing skill.
- **"Leave the rod out and wait" means during a session, while you work another line.** Passive lines also keep ticking while the game is closed: a hooked fish is saved as hooked and waits for you. Passive lines do not earn money offline on their own; you still have to land and sell. **Every line stays out when you go ashore**: walking the strip, hunting and mining pull nothing in (only sailing and changing spot do), the set and trophy lines soak on, and the rod's bite waits for you: a hand line whose moment comes while you are away is pushed a few seconds on at every tick, so it bites within about four seconds of your coming back to the water (the first version multiplied the wait by a thousand by mistake and the rod never bit again) (`tick` when `MODE !== 'game'`, and `settleLines` on load), so you never come back to find the rod reeled in.
- **The world is a branching chart of very different islands, revealed by neighbours and rumors.** Boat tiers: raft 1, skiff 2, cutter 3, trawler 4, clipper 5, ironclad 6, starship 7 (space). **Distance matters too**: every boat has a `reach` in chart pixels (raft 45, skiff 60, cutter 80, trawler 105, clipper 135, ironclad 175, starship anywhere; about 30 px is a day's sail), and a voyage needs both the island's tier and the island within reach of where you are (`withinReach`, `boatReach`, `sailDays`), so the farther islands of a tier wait for the next boat and you hop island to island. The chart draws a dotted brass ring of the current boat's reach around you, the legend says *N days out, too far for your raft* and dims those rows, and the sail button says why. A headless check hops the whole world: with these reaches every island of tier t is reachable on boat t (the minimum that still connects everything is 24 px now that the sixth expansion has filled the sea to its edges); from the shoal the raft reaches eight of the fifteen tier-1 islands, Gull Harbor at the edge. Re-run that check when moving an island or changing a reach. Every tier has two islands and routes cross between them, so there are always several paths. An island is hidden until it is linked to somewhere you have been (then a fogged `???` you can still sail to if your boat reaches) or until a shopkeeper names it in a rumor (then its name and rumor line show, marked with a star). Shopkeepers from tier 3 on (Old Sajan at the delta, and everyone beyond) hear about the exotic islands first. Rod → bigger fish → money → boat → range → new islands → rarer fish is still the ladder.
- **Every island is its own culture, One Piece style, and they get more exotic with distance.** Tiers 1–4 are real places with real fish and animals: a salt-traders' cay, a lantern-lit river wharf, a monsoon delta on stilts, a drowned city's reef, a frozen fjord. Tier 5 on is exotic with invented fish and game: a volcano island whose fish swim in fire, an atoll of water like air, a lake inside a mountain, shoals under a storm that never moves. Space is tier 7. The current set is in `LOCS`; the bank of ideas for more is below.
- **Each island is a strip of four screens you walk:** the shaft head on the far left, then the hunting gate, the village, and the fishing shore on the right (the normal fishing scene); an island with no mine is three (`isleScreens`). Each has one thing to press: the shaft head down into the mine, the gate into the wild, the village building (the shop, the traders' tent, or your camp), and the fishing spot. **Pressing one walks you in first** (`startEntry`, `ISL.auto`, `T.entryMs` 900 ms): the walker steps to it under his own power while the buttons wait, in by the island's own gate the way that gate is entered (through it, up its steps, up or down its ladder: the eleventh pass), fading in at the door of the shop or the tent, then `fadeTo` the hunt, the mine or the sheet; the strip resumes with him standing where he went in. **The way down a mine is the cart** (asked for: "player hopping into the minecart then it descending down"): he walks to the near side of the ore cart on the shaft head's rails, hops over its side in an arc (the load of rock tips out in a few flying bits), stands in it with the cart's wall hiding his legs, and after a beat it rolls along the rails to the way in, wheels turning and him jolting, and there goes down: into the dark of a mouth, clipped to it, or on the lift of a shaft seen in section (the entrance is the island's own since the eleventh pass: `pitSprite`); then the cut to the mine. Numbers in `T.cartRide` (hop 340 ms, wait 120, roll 700, sink 520; `lift` is how high his feet stand in the cart, `drop` how far it sinks). `startEntry` sets `ISL.auto = { kind: 'mine', home, cx, x, at }`, `stepIsle` walks him to the cart and then keeps time from `at` (the moment he reached it), `drawCartEntry` draws cart, man and lift live over the scenery layer from the painted entrance's own geometry (`pitSprite(l, W).G`: the clip, the dark, how far it sinks, the lift), and `drawPit` leaves its own cart and lift out while the ride is on (`cartSprite` is the one cart for both, `T_PIT.cart` its place). He is put back at the way in for when he climbs out. An automatic walk now uses the walk cycle too (`ISL.auto.moving`, `isleFrame`), where it used to slide the standing frame. At a gate he goes in the way the gate says (`wildSprite(l, W).entry`): `through` (he walks through and fades), `stair` (a step up for every step along), `climb` (hand over hand up a ladder at `T.climbSpeed`) or `down` (he sinks behind the ground line). **The strip is drawn to the walker's scale** (asked for: "those stairs are way too small in comparison to the player… get rid of most of the empty space at the bottom"): in island mode `layout()` puts the ground line just above the panel (`ISLE_LAYOUT`, `T.islePanel`; 78% of the height in landscape) so there is no dead band of earth under his feet, and everything that stands on the strip is painted at the walker's own scale, one pixel to the pixel: the ground and what stands about the village (`groundSprite`, the tenth pass), the village building (`drawVillage`, the ninth), and since the eleventh pass the gate with the edge of the hunting ground and the mine's entrance (`wildSprite`, `pitSprite`), so a shack is taller than the man walking past it, an oak is five times his height, a gate is something you walk through and the headland is a cliff. (They were drawn by kind at twice size until then, in a scaled space, `isleScenery` at `T.sceneZoom`: both are gone.) Behind the land stands the island's own landscape. The brass marks stand above the taller scenery. The shop is no longer a tab; you walk to it. Walking is the only character movement in the game and it is deliberately short: `T.walkSpeed` (72 logical px/s, about eight seconds across the strip) and a four-frame walk cycle at `T.walkFps` (`walkA` contact with the back heel lifted and the arms swung, `stand` raised a pixel as the legs pass, the mirrored `walkC`, `stand` again; `isleFrame`). You leave the fishing shore for the strip with the green **EXIT** button at the far right of the shore's tab bar (`#tWalk`, `.tabs button.exit`); the strip's own tabs are Map, Index, Bag, Menu, and the Menu sheet also carries a Map button, so the chart is never more than two taps away.
- **Spots are kinds of water, not just docks, and each one looks it** (the twelfth pass: its own view of the island, its own water, its own bed and what stands in it). Every spot has a `kind` (shore, pier, river, lake, reef, deep water, rocks, kelp, lagoon, cave, lava, hot spring, salt flat, ice, mangrove, weed, ruins, open sea; names in `KIND_NAME`), shown as a brass label in the Spots sheet. Every fishing island has a fifth spot of kind `open` with `boat: true`: open water fished from your own boat, locked until you have one (the raft counts), with the island's big travellers and rarities at higher weight and a few commons so set lines still hook (it is offshore fishing now, with its own rules: see the offshore bullet). On the water the scene swaps the shore for your boat on deep water with the island a haze behind you (`drawOpenWater`).
- **Offshore is its own way to fish: slower, and rarer** (asked for: "go offshore fishing with ur boat, and fish are typically longer to catch but more likely to catch rare fish"). The open-water spot is now called **Offshore** (`KIND_NAME.open`) and everything about it is in `T.offshore`. **Every wait is × `wait` (1.8)**: the rod's bite (6–16 s becomes 11–29 s) and the mean soak of the set and trophy lines (`waitFor`, `passiveMean`, so `soakBonus` stays honest). **The pool is the rarer fish**: the island's uncommon, rare and epic fish from its other waters on the sea come out there too (`offVisitors`: every non-common, non-legend fish at a spot that is not `inland` (river, lake, pond, marsh, hot spring, cave, crater), at a spot weight of `visit`, so a river fish stays in its river and a legend at its one spot); every fish weighs × `tiers[rank]` (1, 2, 4, 6 for common to epic); the commons together are held to `commonShare` (45%) of the pool, the rest getting at least `perKind` (15%) per species up to the other 55%; and `luck` (0.6, + `boatLuck` 0.12 for every boat past the raft, since a bigger boat goes further out) is added to the rod's and the bait's. The pool's total weight is kept, so strays and break-offs keep their share. The size roll leans up a little (`tall`, f → f^(1/1.25)). Bait and line still gate everything: a rare fish that needs squid needs squid, and one too heavy for the line still breaks off, more often out there because the fish are bigger. Sim over eight islands, stick rod, 40 lb line, rod on squid: at the shore 74% common, 26% uncommon, under 1% rare; offshore 28% common, 58% uncommon, 7% rare, 7% epic, mean worth per catch $460 → $1,006, break-offs 1% → 19%, wait 11 → 20 s; on scraps uncommon goes from under 1% to 10% and worth $63 → $77, so offshore pays with bait and not without; the set line on squid $261 → $612 at 107 s a hook against 60; the trophy line on squid $566 → $1,190, one catch in ten epic. **It is one tap away**: with a boat, the panel carries a blue button above the inventory bar, *YOUR SKIFF · Go offshore* at the shore and *OFFSHORE · SKIFF · Back to shore* out there (`renderExtra`, `.tool.sea`, the `spot` action; `S.shoreSpot` remembers the shore spot you left, cleared by a landfall; saves are `v: 17`), the toast says *Offshore: slower bites, rarer fish*, the rig note says it in blue while you are out, the Spots sheet's row and note say it, and a fish landed there carries an **OFFSHORE** pill on the card (the catch carries `off`). Never in the prologue.
- **Descriptions hint; they never list.** A spot's "known for" line, the tide line, a keeper's rumor and an island's blurb describe the water and what it feels like, not the roster: "something coils in the lantern light after dark", never "conger at night". The tide line (`waterText`) reads the pool's shape (a heavy share, a rarity, a legend in its window, a stray) into a line that rotates with the hour. The index and the catch card are where names live.
- **Every island has 3–5 named fishing spots, each known for something.** `spots` on an island: a `kind`, a name, a "known for" line, and a roster of species multipliers (`boat: true` on the open-water spot). Rosters overlap (the commons appear at several spots) but every spot has fish the others lack, and each legend lives at exactly one spot. The pool is spot-based: weight = the species' island weight × the spot multiplier, so a spot with no entry for a fish never gives it. Spots are picked from a sheet (the Spots tab on the fishing screen, or the fishing spot on the island strip); moving pulls in your lines. The current spot shows in the HUD clock line. **Pressing an island on the chart selects it** (`mapClick` maps the press through the letterbox `object-fit: contain` leaves around the canvas, which had thrown every press off, and scrolls the legend to the row) **and the legend shows the tiers**: rows are grouped under a brass head per tier (*Tier 1 · your boat reaches it*, *Tier 3 · needs a cutter*) and every row carries its tier beside the name. **The chart says where you have been, and so does its list** (asked for with a photo of the legend, where a visited island was one named row among the others: "make it clear which islands have been visited"). - **The stalk is ours.** A sighting starts far off and the band is narrow. You can **creep closer** up to three times; every step widens the band (×1.45 per step) and raises the animal's **alertness** by its skittishness × the **wind** (rolled per stalk and drawn as drifting seeds: in your face is cheap, across is normal, at your back costs nearly double) × 2 if the animal has its **head up** (it pauses to look every few seconds, marked on the scene and in red on the panel; creeping then is nearly always fatal). Alertness also climbs on its own and at full it bolts. A point-blank shot is worth more (+10% per step) and the taken card says the distance and the wind. All numbers in `T_HUNT`: the band is the weapon's `acc` × (`bandBase` 1.3 − the animal's diff), the mark swings with a period of `swing[0]` − `swing[1]` × diff seconds (1.8 − 1.0) and at `holdSlow` (0.45) of that while you hold, breath lasts `breath` (3.2 s), the temper climbs over `alertMs` (11 s less 5 s × diff) and a step costs `creepCost` (0.13 + 0.22 × diff) before wind, look and cloak; these were eased once from 1.15 / 1.5−0.95 / 0.55 / 2.6 s / 9−4.5 s / 0.16+0.24 when the first stalks felt too tight. Push your luck or take the long shot: that is the decision every stalk asks. **The bar follows the animal**: the band is × (0.65 + 0.7 × size) and the swing's period × (0.8 + 0.5 × size) × (1.15 − 0.45 × value), where size is 0 for a 10 g animal and 1 for a tonne and value 0 at $20 and 1 at $5,000, both log scale (`sizeF`, `valueF`, `swingPeriod`, `T_HUNT.sizeBand`/`sizeSlow`/`valueQuick`), so a big animal is a big slow target and a dear one is quick; the sighting line says so in a word (*quick*, *slow*, *nimble*, *steady*). **Once in a while the whole flock comes down**: one stalk in twenty (`T_HUNT.flockOdds`) on game up to 60 kg is a flock of three to six (`flockN`); the toast says so, the bar turns brass and breathes (`.hbar.flock`), the panel heads *A FLOCK · n left* in gold, the rest stand behind the one in the band, the band is ×1.4 (`flockBand`), and every animal taken leaves another standing where it fell with its own size and price (`rollSize`, alertness up by `flockAlert` each time) until a miss or a bolt scatters them all; each taken card carries a *Flock · k of n* pill. It is the lucky moment the hunt was missing. **The shot flies**: `loose` decides the hit at once but shows it only after `T_HUNT.flightMs` (380 ms), during which `HU.proj` holds the mark and the animal still and `drawShot` draws the thing itself arcing from the weapon in the hunter's hands up to the animal (or wide past the mark): a stone from the sling, a shaft with a red fletch from a bow, a bolt, a spear, a dart, a ball with a muzzle flash and a smoke trail from a gun, a tumbling throwing stick, by the weapon's icon class; a burst where it lands, white on a hit, dust on a miss; then the card or the bolt. **Overhunting**: every animal taken puts a point of pressure on its species on that island (`S.hunted[loc][id] = { n, t }`, saves are `v: 15`, a v14 save gets an empty record) that fades over `T_HUNT.recoverMs` (ten minutes real time a point, so a hard hour's hunting is back in about an hour); `gamePool` weighs a species × `pressDrop` (0.55) a point, so what you keep taking comes less and the rest of the ground comes instead, and `startStalk` stretches the wait by `waitPress` (0.3) per point of the whole ground's pressure up to `waitPressMax` (×3.5), so a hunted-out ground is slow to show anything. The panel's idle line says it (*something was taken here lately* from one animal taken, *the ground is thinning* from three, *hunted out: let it rest* from six: `T_HUNT.thin`, 0.5, 2.5 and 5.5 points, half a point under the count because a point starts fading the moment it is made; with the first values of 1, 3 and 6 a single fresh kill read as nothing and three as one). Fabled creatures carry none; they are once-ever anyway. **The tracker's tally shows how thin the game is** (asked for with a photo of that line on the panel: "make it where you can buy a tool that gives you a bar that lets you view how thin the game is"): a tool bought once (`TALLY`: $450, a notched stick on a knotted cord, on the shelf of every shop until you own it; `S.tally`, saves are `v: 18`, a v17 save has none) that puts a bar on the hunting ground while you stand idle or wait for a sign (`thinBar`, `groundThin`): **GAME ON THE GROUND**, full when nothing has been taken and empty where the wait stops growing (`waitPressMax / waitPress`, 8.3 points), green, brass, orange or red with a word at its end (*plenty*, *taken lately*, *thinning*, *hunted out*: `THIN_WORD`), a notch where each word turns, and under it what the pressure costs and when it is gone (*Signs come ×2.3 slower · fresh again in 26 min*, the minutes being its most hunted animal's points × `recoverMs`) and the animals it lies on, the scarcest first (*Scarce: Japanese Hare, Sika Stag and 1 more*: from `T_HUNT.scarce` 0.5 points, `thinShow` 2 named). The two hundred and seventy-six newer islands (eighteen in the first expansion, twenty-two in the second, seventeen in the third, twenty-five in the fourth, twenty-eight in the fifth, which grew the chart, a hundred and sixty-six in the sixth, which grew it again) are generated from compact data (one block per island: scene, village, hunting ground, keeper, four spots and an open-water spot, six to eight fish, a legend, three to five creatures) and land in `LOCS`, `SPECIES`, `FISH_ART`, `GAME` and `LEGEND_LORE` as plain entries like everything else. The second expansion added the ground kinds `steppe`, `wrecks`, `reeds` and `cloud`, the props reed, windmill, hull rib, oyster rack, anchor, grave, pages and horse-post, the buildings yurt, cannery, hulk, library, chapel, whitewash dome and turf house, a `paper` shore, a `cloud` weather (a ceiling of cloud for the island that hangs from one) and the water kinds marsh, strait, crater, pond and sky. Islands with no shop have a traders' tent or your camp, and every trader is a named keeper with a face too (Hemi the Salter, Egg-wife Dora, Diver Kesh, Pilgrim Ott, Tonger Bess, Doctor Lind, Captain Rook, Old Nan), reached from the inventory sheet; Gallows Cove buys at double (`sellMult: 2`, applied to the haul and to a single fish; bounties override it). **A real species that already lives somewhere else is the same fish on a new island, never a copy**: the fourth and fifth expansions' emitters turn any fish whose name is already in `SPECIES` (or already written earlier in the same expansion) into a second `loc` on the existing entry (hake at Pot Row, porbeagle off Fogbank, mackerel at three southern islands: ninety-three of them so far) and do the same for a creature already in `GAME` (two hundred and seventy-three multi-home creatures now, most of them from the seventh pass), so the index never lists one animal twice; the per-island minimums (six fish, three creatures) count those second homes.
- **Every keeper has a face, and it is theirs alone.** `FACES[locId]` is a portrait descriptor (skin, hair style and colour, eyes, brows, nose, mouth, beard, hat, collar, extras, and a `kind` for the ones that are not plain humans: `giant` for Brakka under his horned iron helm with glowing eye slits, `frog` for King Brek, `automaton` for Unit Seven, `paper` for the Underlibrarian, `glow` for Nix, `salt` for Salt Ama, `ink` for Scrivener Oss, `shadowed` for the Lamplighter in his hood, `flipped` for Keel Harrow who hangs upside down). `drawFace` paints a 48×48 bust from it at one pixel per pixel, lit from the upper right with the same five tones as the fish and beasts (outline, shadow, base, light, rim), over a round window in the island's colour; `faceC` caches it at 3×, `drawFaces(el)` fills `<canvas class="face">` after a sheet renders, and `keeperBox` lays out portrait, name, TALK and a parchment speech box for what they said. Nothing is drawn by hand and nothing is copied: the parts (fourteen hair styles, twenty-odd hats, collars, beards, scars, kohl, spectacles, a bird on the shoulder, a cup of tea) are composed per keeper, so each island's keeper looks like their island: a tattooed diver, a dyer in a purple turban, a steppe falconer with an eagle, a hermit in a grey headscarf, a ticket-taker with hollow eyes. **Their expressions change as you talk** (`EXPR`, `withExpr(f, expr)`): `neutral` when the sheet opens, then each TALK settles on a face that fits the line (a plain line cycles smile → laugh → sly → frown; naming an island is `surprise` with raised brows and an open mouth; the starship whisper is `sly`), and the mouth moves for a moment first (`animateTalk`: six frames at 140 ms alternating an open mouth with the expression; `faceC` caches per expression, the face canvas carries `data-expr`). Faces with fixed features keep what is theirs: a frog, an automaton, glowing or hollow eyes and a pipe change only what they can. Look at the whole set on a contact sheet (render every `FACES` entry at 3×) before adding one.
- **Sailing is a cutscene, and every landfall is the island's own.** Sail from the chart and `startSail` runs three beats on the cutscene overlay (`MODE === 'sail'`, tap to advance, SKIP to land, Space or Enter on a keyboard): *Casting off.* (the shore you were on, your boat pulling away with you seated in it, a wake behind), *N days under sail.* (a wide shot: the boat small on open water, gulls, the day turning, the island you left sinking behind and the next one rising ahead, each its own skyline (`isleFar`: see the landscapes section), something breaching alongside once; days from chart distance), then the new island's title (its shore mirrored onto the right, the boat easing in) while the island does what it does: `ARRIVE[locId]` names one of twenty-five arrival kinds drawn by `arriveFx`: `wave` (villagers run down and wave, in the island's colours; on rooftops at the flooded city), `birds` (the shore lifts off: gulls, gannets, flamingos, ravens, pages), `erupt` (the volcano answers with ash, lava bombs and a red sky), `lanterns` (lights come on one by one; floating lamps at the ghats, a green one at the ferry), `bell` (rings ride out over the water), `steam` (vents, a geyser), `lightning`, `aurora`, `cannon` (a salute and a near miss), `riders` (horsemen gallop the shore), `hop` (frogs), `glowfish` (lights under the water), `fog`, `gears`, `swarm` (bats, moths), `eyes` (something watches, blinking), `rocket` (the gantry lights up), `leap` (the island's fish come up to look), `dhows` (sails crossing), `spout` (a whale blows and shows its back), `whirl`, `shimmer` (the shore wobbles in the heat), `rainup`, `shooting` (stars fall toward the crater), `sandfall`. `finishSail` then lands you (visited, order, first spot, the landfall toast). - **The spaceport is its own island, hidden, far, and findable only by the chart.** The Gantry (`spaceport: true`, `noFish: true`, tier 6, bottom-right corner of the world, no `links`) never appears as a neighbour's `???`. Landing there goes straight to the island strip, which is one screen of concrete with the hangar and the gantry; no shore, no gate, no fish, no game. Director Hale sells exactly one thing, the Starship, and only to someone already under an ironclad. Every other shop lists the Starship as LOCKED with a hint that a dock exists somewhere. Two ways to learn of it: a keeper on a tier-5+ island, at `T_WORLD.starRumor` (7%) per TALK, lowers their voice about a ship that sails up (no location, sets `S.heardStars`); and **a mysterious chart** at `T_WORLD.chartPrice` ($150,000) that is on a tier-5+ shop's shelf at `T_WORLD.chartOdds` (12%) per island per real day (`chartInStock`, a fixed hash so it does not flicker). Buying it marks the spaceport `known`, which lights it on the chart and lets an ironclad sail there. The Stars stay locked for now; the Starship purchase is the visible endgame step.
- **Shops differ.** Every shop sells the basics (its own rod, line, weapon and pick, made there and sold nowhere else: the island-made gear bullet; a line slot, the trophy rig, the tracker's tally until you own one, bait, shot, the next boat) and five of nine optional shelves (buckets, cloaks, boots, game bags, lamps, ore sacks, charges, mine aids, calls; `SHOP_SHELVES`, `T_WORLD.shopShelves`), chosen by a fixed hash of the island (`shopStock`) so a port's shelves never change and neighbouring ports differ; the shop's note names what is on the shelves here and what to find at another port. The first-island tutorial's buy step only needs the basics. **Shelves hold so much, and every item says what is left**: bait, shot, calls, charges and aids come by the pack, so many a game day per shop (`T_WORLD.stock`: bait 4–9, shot 3–6, calls, charges and aids 2–4, a fixed hash of the shop, the item and the day; `shopLeft`, `shopSold`, `S.stock[loc] = { day, sold }`, saves are `v: 16`), the item's description begins *4 packs left on the shelf today* with the same count in green by the price, a pack bought is gone until the next game day and the row reads SOLD OUT in red (*more comes with the morning boat*); gear is one piece (*1 on the shelf*). **The chart remembers what a landed island has**: every visited row in the legend carries a tag beside its tier, *shop* in green, *traders only · no shop* in brass or *no market* in grey, for good.
- **Every shop has a bounty board.** `boardOf(l)` rolls 0–4 wanted posters for the island's own fish (never the legend) when the shop is first opened, each paying worth × 3–6 once (`T_BOUNTY`), and re-rolls after a real day. Posters are paper cards nailed to the board with the fish's sprite; TURN IN hands over the most valuable matching fish in the bucket. - **Fish are not strictly island-exclusive.** A species can live on several islands outright (`loc` with more than one island: sardine, bream, mackerel, largemouth bass…), and any common fish can stray: on the rod or trophy line it can turn up one tier away from home, on the same side of the exotic line, and the strays together take a fixed sliver of the spot's resident weight (`STRAY.share`, 4%, split among them, so the share stays the same however many islands feed it; `straysTo`). Strays are tagged FAR FROM HOME on the card, never show in the "biting" line, and never come on a set line. Rare fish, legends and mutants do not stray.
- **Rarity sets the price, almost directly, and the top tiers vary the most** (asked for: "rarity should be much more, almost directly correlated to value, with the most variety in value at the top tiers, like the one of a kind fish"). Before this pass a Common ray sold for $400, a Rare minnow for $12, a Fabled creature for less than an Epic one, and every fabled creature and relic of a tier for the same sum. Now everything is priced from `T_VALUE`: a species' typical worth is `unit[kind][its home tier]` (a middling common thing of that tier: fish 22, 24, 30, 34, 38, 62, 105; game 14, 32, 62, 140, 240, 340, 480; minerals 9, 11, 16, 26, 44, 75, 130) × `mid[rarity]` (common 1, uncommon 4, rare 16, epic 80, legendary 560, fabled and relic 380), and the species of one tier and one rarity are spread over hi/lo = `spread[rarity]` around that (2, 2.5, 3.5, 4, then 10 for legend fish and 5 for fabled creatures and relics), each placed by where its own `rate` put it among those peers (`worthTable`, built once; equal rates are spread by a hash of the id, which is what gives the once-ever creatures and relics their variety). So the bands are 0.7–1.4, 2.5–6.3, 8.6–30, 40–160 units and then 177–1,771 (legends) or 170–850 (fabled, relics): they never overlap, commons of a tier are all worth about the same, and one legend can be worth ten times another (tier 1 legends $4k–$43k, tier 6 $19k–$186k; fabled creatures $5k–$27k at tier 1 and $82k–$408k at tier 6; relics $2k–$9k and $22k–$110k). `rate` no longer prices anything by itself; it only orders a species among its peers. The specimen matters a little: × `size[0]` (0.7) at the bottom of its size range to × `size[1]` (1.4) at the top (`valueOf(sp, f, …)` takes the length fraction now; `gameValue` and `mineralValue` work it out from the weight), ±15% rolled at catch time, × 1.5 at trophy size, never under $3; bait worth, variants, shot and the point-blank bonus multiply on top as before. **A fish's rarity now counts what it takes to tempt it** (`rarityOf`): its scarcity in its water (island weight 16+ → 0, 7+ → 1, under 7 → 2) plus its bait tier (shrimp 1, squid 2) plus one for the `rare` flag (never less than rare), capped at epic; so scrap fish are Common, the fish shrimp brings are Uncommon or Rare and the squid fish Rare or Epic, which is what makes paid bait pay now that a Common is cheap whatever took it (1,592 common, 173 uncommon, 424 rare, 106 epic, 289 legendary). Creatures and minerals keep their rarity rules. Income is about where it was (sim, rod, the line a player of that tier holds, all shore spots: on scraps $32 a catch at tier 0 to $167 at tier 6, on shrimp $170 to $1.5k, on squid $293 to $3.9k; a take on the hunt $16 to $1.9k; a find in the mine $44 to $300 at levels 1–3 and $147 to $883 at 4–6, which is three or four times what the deep islands' mines paid before), but it comes from the rare things and not from big common ones: on squid at tier 3 the commons are half the catches at $93 and the rares a quarter at $1,400. Offshore still doubles a catch's worth on bait (rod on squid, eight islands: $498 at the shore, $1,128 offshore; 26% rare or better against 58%). ## Presentation decisions (second pass)

- **The scene is the whole screen.** One fixed canvas fills the viewport; the HUD, the tide line and the control panel float over it. No screen-in-a-screen. The panel is translucent so the water shows through.
- **Logical resolution adapts to the device.** `layout()` picks an integer scale (phone: 2 → about 195×375 logical px; desktop: 3–4) so pixels stay crisp. The surface line sits at 40% of the height in portrait, 52% in landscape. Everything is positioned relative to `W`, `H`, `SURF` and `shoreW`; never hardcode pixel positions. **A phone held sideways gets its own layout**: when the window is wider than it is tall and no taller than 540 px, `layout()` sets `html.land` and the CSS under it turns every mode's container into a row: the HUD pins over the scene's top left, the panel (`#panel`, `#ipanel`, `#hpanel`, `#mpanel`) becomes a 360 px column on the right that scrolls on its own, sheets and the catch card take the whole height (the card scrolls inside its overlay), and toasts stay over the scene. The script keeps `SW`, the scene width beside the column (`W` otherwise), and places the action in it: `shoreW` is 34% of `SW`, the floats spread to `SW` (`floatX`), the hunt's animal and aim mark run across `SW`, the mine's face wall stands at `SW`, and the strip's camera centres the walker in `SW`; backgrounds still fill `W`. Rotating the phone fires `resize`, so it switches live either way.
- **Own look, not One Cast's.** Dithered sky and sea gradients (2×2 Bayer, cached per hour), a big seated fisherman (24×30) with a wide hat and beard, a sun that rises over the water on the right and sets behind the island on the left, light shafts, a sandy seabed that darkens with depth, per-location foregrounds (beach with wreck and palm, pier with shack and lantern, rocks with kelp, your own boat at the trench, an ice shelf with aurora and snow). The map is a parchment chart with ink islands, dotted routes and a compass, not a world map.
- **The title is a wooden sign.** `WOOD_FONT` + `woodTitle` build CASTAWAY from plank blocks on the canvas (alternating plank tones, a seam along the top of each plank, grain streaks, brass nails at plank and post ends, a drop shadow) No DOM logo.
- **The title screen is a screensaver, and the island in it is the island of the story.** The land far off on the right is the shoal's own skyline (`drawIsleFar(LOC.shoal, …)`, mirrored: the palms and the wreck on the bar), the same one the storm's drift beat shows him drifting toward and the drowned morning shows him never reaching. `titleBeat` runs a timed cycle (wait of varying length → bite → reel in → bait up → cast → settle): the float bobs and ripples, dips on the bite, the rod bends and the float comes in to the tip, the rod goes upright while a bait pixel appears on the hook, the float arcs back out and splashes. Every other cycle a small fish jumps right of the boat. Everything is a function of time, so it costs nothing and never desyncs.
- **New voyage starts with a title card, then a playable prologue, then the storm.** A card first (`MODE === 'intro'`): the empty sea at dusk, a skiff far off, the line *Somewhere in the ocean…*, about four seconds or a tap. Then the player begins in the skiff on a calm open sea (hidden location `sea`, never on the chart, its own deep seabed) with the rod only: no map, no shop, no money, one line card. **The prologue is the tutorial**: `tutorialText` turns the tide line into a brass instruction box that answers "what does the game want right now" from state (cast → watch the float → reel on the hit → hold to lift the net → keep or release → one more), the Cast button pulses when it is the player's move, and `rollCatch` forces the first fish to be an easy one (difficulty ≤ 0.2, the second ≤ 0.32) so the first fight teaches. **The prologue's fights are easy but not quick**: the bar starts near empty (`T_FIGHT.prologueStart`), fills at a third of the normal rate (`prologueGain`) and drains at half (`prologueDrain`) but never below a sliver (`prologueFloor`), so a first fish takes eight to ten seconds for a steady hand, a fumbler lands it in eleven to eighteen, and the tutorial fish cannot be lost (sim: 100% at 250 ms, 98–100% for a fumbler before the floor; in-game a player who never follows the shadow still lands both, in 10 and 25 s). The real cast/bite/fight loop runs. When the second fish is kept or released, "The light is going." and the cutscene starts. Six beats, each tappable, skippable, captions kept to a few words and left unexplained: *No warning.* (storm) → *He did not let go.* (drift) → *Morning. Somewhere.* (washed up, `beachScene` mode `lie`) → *Nobody is coming.* (he sits up where he lay, the rod still on the sand behind him: `up`) → *Sea, every way he looked.* (on his feet, facing the water: `stand`) → *He stuck to what he knew.* (on the plank with the rod: `fish`, which is the game's own first frame, so the cut into play is seamless). The narration stays third person, past tense, and never explains. **The storm asks one question.** Partway into the first beat a brass box asks *Hold on to your rod?* with Hold on / Let go; the beat waits for the answer (tapping elsewhere does nothing, SKIP counts as holding), and the second caption answers it: *He did not let go.* or *He let go.* The answer is kept in `S.heldRod`. **Let go and you drown.** The cutscene branches (`cutBeats()` picks `CUT_DROWN` over `CUT`): *He let go.* (he sinks through dark water, bubbles rising, the rod drifting off alone: `drawSink`) → *The sea kept him.* (a calm morning, only his hat on the swell: `drawKept`, the `hat` sprite) → a black card, *Drowned.*, with the hint *tap to start again*. `endCut` then wipes the save (`freshState`) and shows the title with no Continue and the line *He let go of his rod, and he paid the price.* under NEW VOYAGE, so the player starts the whole prologue again. There is no no-rod start any more (the whittle path is gone; an old save that had let go gets its rod back on load). Hold on and nothing changes. When it ends the save resets to the shoal with no boat, empty bucket and one line, the rod; the index keeps the two prologue fish, so the story is in the record. Closing the game mid-storm resumes the storm. Continue skips all of it. The menu replays the cutscene only.
- **The first island after the shoal teaches the village and the wild, once.** `S.tut` is a stage machine (`exit → village → talk → sell → buy → gate → hunt → mine → done`) started by `finishSail` on the first landfall anywhere but the shoal (`tutStart`, `S.tutIsland`), and ended early if the player sails away. On the shore the green **EXIT** glows (`.exit.pulse`, `@keyframes beaconG`) and the tide line says to go ashore; on the strip the hint box turns brass and walks the player ◀ to the village (`tutStripText`; the gate is left, the village middle, the shore right); pressing the door opens the shop with a brass box at the top (`tutShopText`): TALK first, then **Sell the haul** (the shop counter now always has a sell button; the traders' tent uses the inventory sheet's), then buy something if anything is affordable (closing the shop without buying moves on too, so nobody is trapped); then the strip walks the player ◀ to the gate and the hunt's note line teaches by phase (`tutHuntText`: wait for a sign, keep still, hold DRAW and let go inside the band, creep for a wider band); the first stalk's end, taken or missed, sends the player on ◀ to the shaft head (`tutFinishHunt`), where the mine's note line teaches by state (`tutMineText`: strike a tile, read the glints, pry the opened stone, hold PRY and let go in the band) and the first find or the climb out finishes the whole lesson with a toast (`tutFinishMine`); an island with no mine ends the lesson after the stalk. A sells-only first island skips the buy step; an island with nothing goes straight to the gate. `tutHere`/`tutAt`/`tutSet` are the helpers.
- **Captions crossfade** (`setCap` swaps the text only after the fade-out) and do not use `text-wrap: balance`, which drew ghost glyphs on iOS Safari. iOS can also leave a ghost of the previous caption at its old position once the choice box moves it; the caption is its own compositing layer (`will-change`, `translateZ(0)`) and `setCap` flips `display` off and on at the swap to force a repaint.
- **Every voyage has an eight-digit code, and the code is the save.** Before a run starts the title asks you to choose a code (`codeBox('new')`: type eight digits, tap Random, or tap one of **three suggested codes that are easy to remember and confirmed free** (`memorableCodes` ranks a straight run, a block said twice or mirrored, a year twice, round thousands, shuffled within each tier; `suggestCodes` skips any living voyage on this device or in the cloud, at most fourteen cloud checks); the code box is on the title screen and takes Enter and Escape on a keyboard). `SYNC.testCodes` (`00000000`) are the designer's test voyages: a new voyage on one always restarts it, cloud copy included, with no drowning toast. A code with a **living voyage** under it anywhere (a save on this device or in the cloud with `started` and not `drowned`; `isLive`, `cloudLive` reads the `started` leaf) is refused, so nobody can start a new run on someone's code. **A drowned voyage frees its code**: the dead save is written with `started: false, drowned: true`, Enter a code says *That voyage ended… the code is free again*, the code box offers it among the free ones, and a new voyage on it keeps the old `created` stamp (`cloudCreated`) so the database rule lets the new run overwrite the dead one; a toast says the last voyager with the code drowned. **Enter a code** on any device opens that voyage where it was last saved (`fetchSave` takes the newer of the device's copy and the cloud's). Continue shows the current code; the Menu sheet shows it with the cloud state; a save from before codes is asked to claim one on Continue (`'claim'`). Starting a new voyage no longer wipes anything: the old voyage stays under its own code (so the second-tap wipe protection is gone). Storage: the current save still lives under `KEY`, plus a copy per code (`castaway-save-<code>`), `castaway-code` for the current one; every save stamps `S.saved`, a new run stamps `S.created`. **The cloud is a Firebase Realtime Database reached over plain REST** (`SYNC.url`, the project `castaway-976ff`, owned by the designer's Google account; `/saves/<code>.json`; `cloudGet`/`cloudHas`/`cloudPut`, a debounced `cloudPush` after every save and a `keepalive` `cloudFlush` on hide and pagehide; on boot a newer cloud copy replaces the local one while the title is still up; last write wins). With `SYNC.url` empty the whole system still works on one device and the code screens say so. Tests point `SYNC.url` at a mock through `localStorage['castaway-sync-url']` (`mockdb.js` in the scratchpad speaks the four REST calls). Database rules to paste in Firebase: `{"rules":{"saves":{"$code":{".read":true,".write":"!data.exists() || newData.child('created').val() == data.child('created').val()",".validate":"$code.matches(/^[0-9]{8}$/) && newData.hasChildren(['code','created','saved'])"}}}}` (a code can be created once; only a run carrying the same `created` stamp can overwrite it, which is the run itself or the new run that takes over a drowned code; nothing but eight-digit codes). The mock enforces the same rule.

## Landscapes: every island its own country (the ninth pass)

Asked for: "the entire game feels like it's built off of the same template, especially the landscape in the background… make every island have its own unique landscape and intricate, detailed and rich background, every detail should feel special", and "some buildings still aren't changed to scale". Before this the fishing screen was the same wedge of shore under an empty sky on every island, and the strip had the same two sine-wave hills behind it.

- **A landscape is three depth layers painted once and then blitted.** `far`, `mid` and `near` (`T_LAND`: they slide at 0.08, 0.2 and 0.4 of the ground's rate and are drawn at 1, 2 and 4 pixels to the metre), each a pixel buffer (`Paint`: a `Uint32Array` over an `ImageData`, with a second buffer of lights and a list of emitters) filled by the island's own description, `LANDS[id] = s => { … }`. `landScene` builds them when an island is first shown in a mode (30–130 ms on a phone and up to 200 ms in a desktop window under a ×4 CPU throttle; 2–4 MB on a phone, 6–9 MB on a desktop; only the island you are on is kept), `landTint` relights them when the hour changes (each layer hazed toward the sky's lower colour by its depth, `T_LAND.haze`, a band of mist thickening toward the foot of the far and middle layers, night darkening on top, all with `source-atop` so it costs three fills a layer), and `drawLandscape(l, cam, yBase, t, nt, o)` blits them, shows the lights with the night's alpha and runs the emitters. A frame costs 1.3–3.5 ms under the same throttle on every island (measured on all 290: `landall.js` builds the 869 scenes with no error or warning). The fishing view's far and middle layers are built wider than the others (`S.wide`: by `T_LAND.off` screens), because out on open water they slide that much further and a wall that must cover the screen (a cavern's back) would otherwise end in the air.
- **The same country stands behind everything.** `drawGame` (camera at `3W`, the fishing shore; out on open water `o.off` slides the layers further away, hazes them and drops the near one), `drawIsland` (the camera's own x, so walking the strip pans the layers against each other), `drawHunt` (camera at the gate's screen, sea level at the hunt's ground line), `drawDepart` and `drawArrive` (the landfall mirrored, `o.flip`). The old far silhouettes (`scene.far` in `drawShoreGeneric`, the hand-drawn shores' haze shapes, the strip's `drawIsleBackdrop`, the hunt's haze ridge, the open water's haze island) are gone. **A `cave` island's landscape is the cavern itself** (`S.cave`, from `scene.weather === 'cave'`: the swiftlet caves, the mushroom wood, the hollow mountain, the whale's belly, the salt cathedral, the great pearl): a back wall going up out of sight (`caveWall`), pillars, stalactites, shafts of daylight, bamboo scaffolds, and the mouth with the day outside it (`caveMouth`: a hole cleared in a layer, with an emitter marked `under: true` that draws the hour's sky and sea *before* the layer's bitmap). Underground the distance fades toward dark instead of toward the sky (`s.haze = '#hex'` chooses the colour), night does not darken it, and its lamps are always lit.
- **Three things a description may set on the scene.** `s.haze` (the colour the distance fades to: the whale's belly is red inside), `s.lit` (0..1: the lights buffer is shown at least this strongly at any hour, for a cavern and for an open-air island that asks for it: the eclipse, the two moons, the drowned sun, the standing bolt of lightning, the lines the stars fish with) and `s.strip` (read, not set: true on the strip, where the village building itself stands in front, so a landscape leaves its twin out: `if (!s.strip) m.put('lighthouse', …)`). An emitter may be `under: true` (drawn before its layer: a sky seen through a hole, a sun, a darkened sky) and `wide: true` (never culled for being off screen: anything that fills the width).
- **Coordinates, and where the eye can see.** Along a layer `v` is in screen widths: `v` 0..1 is the fishing shore's own view and negative `v` is inland (a layer of rate `f` shows `v` from `(k-3)f` to `(k-3)f+1` with the camera on strip screen `k`: 0 the mine, 1 the gate, 2 the village, 3 the shore; `L.at(k, sx)`). Land should start at or left of `v` −0.3 (far), −0.7 (mid), −1.3 (near) and usually ends near 0.4 (near), 0.5–0.6 (mid), 0.7 (far), leaving the sea its horizon. Heights are fractions of `LH`, the sky between the HUD and the horizon (56–168 px). Sizes of things are metres × the layer's `u`. **The fishing shore and the fisherman cover the left third of the fishing view up to about 0.35 `LH`**, which hid the landmark of a dozen islands in the first drafts: what an island is known for stands at `v` 0.36–0.95 on the middle or near layer, or rises above 0.45 `LH`; and a flat island (a marsh, a salt pan, a sandbank) needs something of its own out on the water on the near layer (a bar, rafts, posts, a wreck, a skerry with one house) or it reads as empty sea. A mass's methods (`trees`, `put`, `town`, `dots` …) hang off what `L.land`, `L.flat` or `L.stack` returns, not off the layer; a `flat` under three pixels high is too thin for trees or animals to stand on.
- **The vocabulary** (the landscape block of `index.html`, between `// ---------- landscapes ----------` and `// ---------- end of landscapes ----------`): `L.land(pts, mat, o)` paints a mass of land from control points `[v, h, how]` (`'s'` smooth, `'l'` straight, `'c'` a cliff at the end, `'r'` a cliff at the start) with `rough`, `crag`, `step`, a `cap` (turf on a cliff, snow above a line, guano), `strata`, in one of forty-odd materials (`MAT`: each a five-tone ramp and a texture: `soft`, `rock`, `cliff`, `dune`, `ice`, `lava`, `flat`), lit from the upper right by slope. On a mass: `trees(kind, a, b, n, o)` (twenty kinds drawn at any size: `TREE`), `forest`, `fields`, `terraces`, `walls`, `path`, `fall` (a waterfall), `river`, `lava`, `creeks`, `rocks`, `patches` (also sails and sheets laid out to dry), `dots(kind, …)` (sheep, cattle, tortoises, penguins, flamingos, egrets, seals, walrus, reindeer, musk ox, bears, crocodiles, albatross, otters… the larger ones drawn in detail on the near layer), `ledges` (birds on a cliff), `climbers`, `kelp`, `cave`, `town(a, b, n, style, o)` (houses up a slope in one of nineteen looks: `TOWN`), `put(kind, v, o)` and `row(kind, a, b, n, o)` for the hundred-odd things in `ST` (church, chapel, lighthouse, windmill, watermill, kiln, stack, smokehouse, warehouse, boathouse, inn, barn, crane, masts, jetty, racks, nets, boat, hull, fort, ruin, stones, cairn, obelisk, statue, totem, torii, pagoda, temple, shikhara, mosque, stupa, yurt, igloo, tent, greenhouse, observatory, mast, poles, bridge, wall, fence, well, cross, gallows, beacon, tank, derrick, haystacks, washing, figure, heap, pans, longshed, steps, bellpost, cart, stall, pots, pit, copper, signal, arch, dish, pylon, dome, engine house, barrels, sacks, skeps, amphorae, a whale's jawbone and ribs, a trypot, a sundial, a cannon, a gibbet, ghats, a great wheel, an elephant, a reed tower, a mill wheel, a great bell, a sledge, a kennel, a beehive, a buried roof, a net house, a great loom, a salt pillar, a heap of hats, a puppet, pigeonholes, a cake, a bookcase, a shrine, a blank sign, a bone tree, and `bldg`/`tower` for anything described in full). On a layer: `ship(v, rig, o)` (nineteen rigs), `fleet`, `sea(kind, v, o)` (buoy, rock, whale, wreck, floats, stilt fishers, posts), `stack` (a sea stack or skerry, with an arch if wanted), `flat` (a sandbank or mudflat), `reeds`, `disc` (a moon or a world), `cloud`, `flock`, `plume`. Buildings are measured in metres (`bldg`, `tower`, `roofOn`, `wallTex`, `win`) and lose their detail as `u` falls, so one description serves every depth. Emitters (`EMIT`: smoke, plume, sails, fall, flag, beam, blink, birds, glint, pulse, wheel, walk, fn) are the only things redrawn every frame. **Beyond the plain country** there are helpers written as the tiers needed them, free functions that take the layer: masonry and harbours (`masonry`, `sentry`, `careened`, `arena`, `bellBarge`, `shipAt`, `spanAt`, `poolOn`, `clockAt`, `sparks`, `swarm`, `creep`), caverns (`caveWall`, `stalactites`, `pillar`, `caveMouth`, `lightShaft`, `scaffold`, `ribArch`), the cold (`geyser`, `steam`, `fogBank`, `glacier`, `berg`, `floes`, `giantTrunks`, `tuskers`, `balloon`, `bore`) and the islands that are not real (`organPipes`, `flameAt`, `twinAbove`, `skullHill`, `lilypad`, `drips`, `shimmer`, `flytrap`, `rings`, `tooth`, `fishRain`, `mothsAt`, `tentacle`, `flight` (a flight of stairs), `vaneAt`, `ropeAlong`/`loopPts`, `bunting`, `digitAt`, `greatWave`, `cogAt`, `darkSky`, `seaFire`, `hangingRock`, `chainLinks`, `sketchRegion` (turns part of a layer into pencil outline on paper), `maskAt`, `upRain`). - **Every island's landscape is written from what the island is: all two hundred and eighty-nine, and the spaceport.** Each `LANDS[id]` is six to twelve lines built from the island's own blurb and vibe. - **The village building is drawn at the walker's own scale, and every island's is its own** (`VILLAGE[form]`, `villageSprite`, `villageAddons`, `drawVillage`, `T_VILLAGE.u` 16 px to the metre: the walker is about 1.85 m under his hat, so a door is 33 px and a shop front is twice his height). - **The way down** (`PITROCK`, `PITIN`, `PITMOUTH`, `PITHEAD`, `pitShaft`, `drawLift`, `cartSprite`): what the entrance is cut into (`in`: a hump of the mine's own rock by daylight, a bluff running off the world's edge, a crag, a bank, a quarry face in benches, a heap, a built wall under an eave, a tower's foot going up out of sight and banded if it is a light, a dome, a stack, or any mass or thing) and how you go in: `form: 'adit'` with a `mouth` (timber, arch, raw, door or iron door, slot with a ladder let down it, megalith, ribs, roots, ring, ice; a `glow` from inside, a `drip`) or `form: 'shaft'`: a hole straight down *seen in section below the ground line*, lined (timber, stone, brick, ice, iron, raw), a ladder on its far wall, a glow at the bottom if it has one, under a `head` (headframe with a turning sheave and a winding drum, gin, windlass, well, iron cage, crane, hatch). A shaft has a lift at the ground the rails run onto; the cart rides it down. A town afloat or on piles gets a plug of real ground for its shaft to be sunk through. Rails, a cart loaded with the mine's own rock (30 x 27, painted), spoil and the island's things about the head.
- **The hunting ground** (`T_HUNTART`, `HFLOOR`, `HGROUND`, `HITEM`, `huntDesc`): a floor that runs away in perspective from the kneeling hunter to the line the animal stands on (rows crowd together toward it; mottled in two colours, dithered, hazed with distance, tinted a little by the island's seed), what lies on that ground growing smaller with distance (tufts, ferns, leaves, stones, flowers, shells, ripples, pools, cracks, plates of crust, flagstones, planks, shards, snow, moss, hummocks), the island's own cover on the far line at `uFar` (20) px to the metre, which is the same trees and faces that stand behind its gate (`back` and `deep`), and something near to kneel behind. A ground gets its kind's floor unless its line names another (`floor: 'moor'`, `'guano'`, `'chalk'`, `'needles'`, `'shell'`, `'saltmarsh'`…: `HGROUND`) or describes its own (`hunt: { c, c2, items, near }`). The taken card's strip of ground is the island's floor colour too.
- **A line can be short.** `wildAuto` gives a ground what its kind gets (so an island with nothing said is still painted), shaped by the island's own style keys: `tree` (a kind or several), `under`, `dense`, `rock`, `side` (where the cliff stands: left, right, both, all) and `cliff`, `sand`, `grass`, `reed`, `stone`, `add` (more behind), `floor`; the sand and the rock default to the island's own shore's (`wildTone`). An explicit `deep`, `back`, `run`, `gate`, `mid` or `front` replaces the kind's. Things are placed by `at` (a fraction of the screen), `dx` (metres from the middle: the pit's things, so a phone shows them), or `n` to the 200 px, so a desktop's wider screen is as full as a phone's.
- **Every island's is written: all two hundred and eighty-nine**, each from its own two lines and looked at beside its neighbours (`wildall.js` counts 289 gate lines and 289 mine lines). The real islands (tiers 0 to 4) are what their words said: a stile in the hedge behind Gull Harbor, a rope ladder up a rock white with gannets, a kissing gate in a drystone wall, a lychgate, a punt on the bank, a headframe over a cockle strand, a door in the foot of a lighthouse. From tier 5 the gate and the way down are whatever the island is: two weeping amber willows, a door in a barrow, steps up the cratered face of a beached moon, ranks of basalt organ pipes with a slot between them, pawns on a chequered floor that runs away in perspective, a tooth the size of a house, a gate between two bobbins as big as houses with the great rope going down beside the shaft, a man-sized door in the door that is the island, a jawbone of ribs going up out of sight inside the whale, roots hanging out of the cloud the island hangs from, threads of light let down by the stars, a standing bolt of lightning beside a gin, tally marks scored down a cliff, a gate half in pencil at the painter's island and all in pencil at the mapmaker's error, an eye in the kraken's mantle beside the cut. One of the game's own creatures can stand on the strip at its own size, shaded like the rest (`['beast', { sil: 'horse' }]`: the hunt's sprite, the island's own animal of that shape if it has one), since the bank's small companions are too small for a horse, a camel or a bear beside the walker; they go in `front`, never in `back`, so they do not stand on the hunt's far line looking like quarry. A ship's side is a mass of its own (`hull`: the sheer rising to bow and stern, laid open where her planking is gone with the ribs standing in the holes, a mast's stump; ports and a stripe if she is iron).
- **What the invented islands needed** (`wild5.js`, `wild6.js`): a mass in ranks of `columns`, each its own height, with `flutes`; masses that `glow`, carry `suckers`, a `tally` in fives or lines of `script`; a great musical `note`, a `bobbin`, a `bolt` of lightning that stayed, an `eye`, and `hang` (anything let down from above the picture to a height: a root, a tarred rope, a chain, a thread of light, a drop held in the air); a light under a gate (`GDECO.glow`); a chequered hunting floor in perspective (`hunt: { check }`); and `post`, what a finished picture may have done to it (`WILDPOST`, on the gate's screen, the mine's and the hunt alike): `mirror` (the ground shows everything again upside down), `sketch` (all of it, or one side of it, turned to pencil on paper), `shade` (one side of a line in shadow), `tint`, `ghost` (half there), `swarm` (midges in the air), `shadows2` (two moons). That is the same trap as a function declared twice (a later name wins silently), one table over.
- **Building and looking.** `land/wild.js` (what grows), `wild2.js` (masses, runs), `wild3.js` (gates, `wildSprite`), `pit.js`, `hunt.js`, `wild4.js` … `wild6.js` (added as the islands needed them, and the named floors), `specs11a.js` … `specs11f.js` (the islands, a file a tier), spliced with the rest by `landbuild.py`. `tri.js ids|tier:t-a-b tag` puts each island's gate screen, mine screen and hunt side by side; `wsheet.js ids wild|pit out.png w` shows the bare pictures enlarged at any width; `ride.js island mine|hunt tag` films the way in frame by frame; `wildall.js` builds all three pictures for every island at two widths and must report no error and no warning (a tree, a gate, a run, a mouth, a head or a floor named in a line that does not exist is a warning); `entryall.js` walks every island's two ways in for real, six pages at once, and reports any that does not arrive (last run: 578 entries on the 289 islands, 234 gates walked through, 29 up steps, 16 climbed, 10 gone down, 120 shafts and 169 adits ridden, none failed; the locked Stars are not an island and are skipped); `bankref.js tier` prints a line an island (its bank's stuff and colours, its vibe) to write a gate and a pit that match the ground they stand on; `evshot.js snippet.js out.png` runs a snippet inside the game's own scope (the test page exposes `window.__EV`). What it costs: a gate's screen takes 6 ms to paint at a phone's width and 11 at a desktop's, a mine's 5 and 7, a hunting ground 14 and 44 (the slowest, a wood at a desktop's width, 160), once per island; a frame costs 0.2 to 0.45 ms in every scene (`cost.js`).

## Every fishing spot its own water (the twelfth pass)

The eleventh pass left one screen still made from a template, and it is the one the player looks at most. The game has 1,153 spots fished from a shore (and 293 offshore), three to five an island, each with a name and a line about its water, and choosing one changed nothing on the screen but a word in the HUD: every spot of an island showed the same view of it, the same water and the same flat bed. Now a spot is a place.

- **Where the eye turns** (`SPOT_VIEW`, `spotViews`, `spotView`). The island's first spot keeps the view its landscape was composed for. Every other spot turns by its kind: inland for a river, a lake, a pond, a marsh, a hot spring or a crater (the camera steps back toward the land by up to `T_SPOT.inland`, 1.5 screens, so the near layer's land runs right across and reads as the far bank), a little inland for a lagoon, a harbour, flats, mangrove; out toward open water for rocks, a channel, kelp, a reef, a strait, deep water (a share of the open-water slide, up to `T_SPOT.out`, 0.8: `drawLandscape`'s `o.off` is a number now, 1 out on open water, and the haze and the slide scale with it). An island's spots are kept at least `T_SPOT.step` (0.24) apart, so no two of them show the same country; on a `cave` island the eye only turns inland, since its near layer is not built to slide out. The casting-off beat of a voyage shows the shore as it was fished from.
- **The water** (`SPOTWATER`, `spotWater`): a tint laid over the island's own (a river brown, a lagoon turquoise, a pond green, deep water near black, a hot spring milky, mangrove water the colour of tea) and how its surface moves (`surf`: how many rows of waves, how fast, how long, how white: a pond barely stirs, a river and a channel run, a strait runs white, ice is still), and whether shafts of light come down (not in a cave, under ice, in a pond or among mangroves). `drawSeaBase` takes it as `o.water`.
- **What stands in it and lies under it** (`spotSprite`, `SpotB`, `SPOTKIND`, `drawSpot`): painted once per spot into a `Paint` buffer `T_SPOT.up` (64) rows taller than the water (a sea cave's takes the whole sky), from the spot's kind, the island's own colours (its bed from `FLOORS[id]`, its rock from `wildTone`, a coral palette by seed, paler on cold islands) and a seed of the island and the spot's id, so two reefs on two islands are two reefs. It replaces the flat `floorLayer` wherever an island's shore is drawn (`drawShoreGeneric`, `drawShore`). The bed has a depth and a shape by kind (`bed`: flats and salt pans 16 to 30 px under the surface, so a phone shows the bottom; a reef 50 to 66; deep water, a strait, a crater and ice fall away out of sight; a channel is a cut between two shoulders with a red can and a green cone on withies) and a texture (sand ripples, gravel, mud, rock, salt crust). On it and in the water by kind: `shore` shells, starfish, eelgrass, pebbles; `pier` a row of old piles, braced, a lamp on the last that lights at night; `harbour` mooring buoys on their chains, a dolphin of two piles, an anchor, a keg and a boot on the mud; `river` gravel bars coming up to boulders with white water trailing from them, reeds at the far side; `lake` weed beds, a sunken log, reeds; `pond` lily pads in flower, duckweed; `marsh` reed clumps standing in the water; `lagoon` pale sand, small corals, glints; `flats` eelgrass, worm casts, withies; `saltflat` crust, salt pillars, rafts of salt afloat; `reef` corals of four kinds and a column of coral rock with its head a few px under the surface; `rocks` the bed coming up in rises to boulders awash, weed on them, foam at their feet; `kelp` fronds to the surface with their floats; `weed` mats afloat with their strands hanging; `strait` standing waves and an eddy; `deep` a wall going down, sometimes a pinnacle or whips on the wall, a long shadow passing below every so often and now and then a fin; `crater` a bowl with its rim standing out of the water; `wreck` ribs, a keel and a mast leaning out of the water; `ruins` columns, some through the surface, fallen drums; `cave` the roof over the water with its stalactites and drips and the far wall coming down into it, in the island's own rock in shadow (not on a `cave` island, whose landscape is the cavern already); `ice` a sheet over the water open where the lines go down, icicles under it, a pressure ridge on it; `lava` glowing cracks and vents on a black bed, bubbles, steam; `hotspring` sinter terraces stepping up to the surface, bubbles, steam; `mangrove` prop roots arching down from trees that go up out of the picture. What stands through the surface gets a wet line and a fleck of foam; everything under water is tinted wetter and dimmer with depth; at night the whole picture dims like the bank (`T_SPOT.night`), its lights show, and the spot's tint goes with the light, so a turquoise lagoon is not turquoise in the dark. A rise in the bed (`rises`: under a boulder, a gravel bar) keeps its slope for its first 36 px and then goes down steeply, so over deep water it is a skerry and not a mountain; what a name does to the bed is applied first and the kind's own shape laid on that.
- **A spot's name adds to its kind** (`SPOTBED`, `SPOTEXTRA`, at most `T_SPOT.extras` things): a *hole*, a *sump* or a *well* is a hole in the bed nobody sees the bottom of; a *ledge*, a *shelf* or a *drop* is an edge the bed falls away from; a *bar* or a *shoal* comes up to a few px under the surface; *shallows* are shallower; *steps* go down from the bank; a *tower*, a *steeple* or a *chapel* stands in the water with its window and its cap; a *bridge* or *arches* are two piers and the ring sprung between them (broken, in ruins); a *bell* lies on the bed; a *lantern* is a lamp on a pile; *moorings*, *pots* and *nets* are floats with what hangs under them; *racks* and *stakes* are withies; a *sluice*, a *mill* or a *race* is a gate at the edge of the picture with water coming through; *roots*, *reeds*, *lilies*, *kelp*, *gardens* grow; *hot*, *warm*, a *spring*, a *kiln* or *chimneys* are vents and steam; *cold*, a *berg*, a *lead* are floes; a *keel*, a *hull*, a *hulk* are ribs; *bones*, a *skull*, a *grave*; *shells*, *mussels*, a *pearl*; a *pillar* or a *stack* is a stack; a *quay*, a *jetty*, a *slip* are piles; an *eddy*; *glass*, a *mirror*, *silver* glint; *black*, *dark*, *ink* are darker water. The table is read top to bottom and a kind that has the thing already does not get it twice.
- **What lives there** (`spotLife`; the real islands only, tiers 0 to 4): a gull on the water at about half the shore, pier, harbour, channel, lagoon and wreck spots (a puffin sometimes where it is cold), a duck or a goose on fresh water, a heron standing in the shallows of flats and marshes (a flamingo on the pink lagoon), a cormorant drying its wings on the first rock. They are the bank's own animals (`CRIT`), their feet under the water.
- **The bank turns** (`bankTurned`, `spotTurn`): the bank is the island's and keeps its form and its seat, but at each spot another of its things stands nearest him (the list is turned by the spot's place among the island's shore spots; a thing fixed by `at` stays), so a phone, which has room for one thing, shows a different one at every spot: another stretch of the same shore.
- **The fish swim as deep as the spot is** (`reseedFish` reads the painted spot's `fish`: no deeper than the bed over flats, down to 96 px where there is none). The reach's four hard-coded kelp fronds are gone; its kelp stands at its kelp spots like any other island's. The trench, fished from your own boat, has no shore for the bed to come up to. The strip's shore screen, a landfall and the storm's last beat draw the island's shown spot (`spotShown`: the one being fished, or its first when the island is seen from elsewhere or the spot is offshore), so the cut into play still changes nothing.
- **Offshore has its own sea** (`SPOTKIND.open`, `drawSpotOpen`, `SPOTWATER.open`): the 293 open-water spots were one picture (your boat on deep water over the same flat bed). Now there is nothing under the boat, a long slow swell on top, and one of the things the open sea shows, by the island's seed: a long shadow passing below and a fin cutting the surface, gulls working over a ball of small fish that turns on itself under them (one bird going down into it now and then), a pinnacle coming up out of the dark, lines of drifting weed, or a whale blowing a long way off. Its name still adds to it (buoys in *roads*, kelp *beyond the kelp*).
- **The fight is fought in this water** (`fightLook`, `fightDress`): the fight's column was the same teal over the same brown bed for every fish in the game, in a sea of fire as in a sea of milk. It takes the island's sea and the spot's tint now (held light enough at the top and dark enough at the bottom that the fish's shadow shows against it all the way down), its bed is the island's own where the water has one in reach, and a shape or two of the spot stands at its edges under the net: kelp swaying, coral, boulders, reed stems, mangrove roots, an ice sheet across the top with the hole in it, bubbles and a glow over lava, a wreck's ribs, a drowned column, a pile.
- **What it costs.** A spot takes 2 ms to paint (the slowest 25), once; the fishing scene costs 0.34 to 0.52 ms a frame with it (`cost.js`). `spotall.js` paints all 1,446 (the 1,153 shore spots and the 293 offshore) at a phone's size and a desktop's and must report no error; `fightshot.js island:spot,... tag` captures the fight in a list of waters; `spotshot.js ids|kind:a,b:n|tier:t-a-b tag [hour] [WxH]` puts spots side by side as the screen shows them (`BARE=1` hides the HUD and the panel, to see the bed a desktop shows); `cuttest.js` runs the storm to its end and puts its last beats beside the game's first frame. The sources are `land/spot.js` (views, water, the painter) and `spot2.js` (the kinds, the names, the life), spliced with the rest.

## The mine's gallery, every island's own (the thirteenth pass)

With the gate, the entrance, the hunting ground and the water done, the gallery was the last scene made from one drawing: a speckled band in the kind's colour on every island, the same props every 44 px, a flat dark above and below, and two islands whose mines were both called *the brick pit* were the same screen. It is painted per island and per level now.

- **One picture a level, painted once** (`mineSprite(l, level)`, `GalB`, `drawMineArt`; `T_GAL`): the whole screen in the island's own rock in section, the stuff its entrance on the strip is cut from (`pitRockOf`, `stuffOf`, the bank pass's close textures), with its beds dipping the island's own way (a seed gives the dip where the kind does not) and another slice of them at every level. The gallery is cut through it: a far wall, flatter and further off, a roof line (rough in bare rock, a run of arches under a vault), the floor with the rail on it (`pitRails`) and the muck at the foot of the face, which is hewn and pick-marked. Kept: three painted levels.
- **The island's own minerals show in the rock** (`veins`): every mineral that lies under the island at this level is there to see, an ore or a stone as a vein wandering along the dip in its own colours with a glint that comes and goes, a gem or a crystal as a pocket, a fossil as itself (its own sprite lying in the bed, half faded into the rock); more of what is commoner, a stray vein of what lies at other levels, never the relic. So the wall says what the mine holds, and it changes as you go down.
- **How it is held up comes from how the island's entrance is built** (`line`; `pitOf`): timber sets with caps, wedges and lagging (83 islands), stone or brick piers under a vault's ring (74: an arched mouth, or any mine whose name is a *cellar*, a *vault*, a *crypt*, a *magazine*, where the rock is real stone), pillars of ice left standing and icicles (8), iron hoops and a pipe run along the roof (17), bone ribs curving over (2), roots coming down through the roof into the floor (8), orthostats under capstones (3), and bare rock with a stull set here and there where the roof wanted one (94).
- **The way back up is at the left** (`way`): on the first level the day itself, by how the island goes in: the mouth of an adit seen from inside, the shaft you came down with its lining and its ladder and the light at the top of it (a well's is round stone with the rope and the bucket), an open cut where the sky comes down to the floor and the rock steps back from it in benches (a *quarry*, a *cutting*, a *dig*), or a flight of steps up to a lit door (a cellar). Below the first level it is a ladderway up through the roof.
- **Light** (`hang`, `light`): everything is lit from the miner's lamp outward and falls away into the dark in dithered steps; three lamps hang in the gallery on the first level, one fewer each level down and none from the fourth, and each level is a little darker than the last, so the deeper you are the more your own lamp is all there is. What gives its own light is painted after the dark: the day at the mouth, hung lamps, lava cracks, crystals.
- **What each kind of rock has in it** (`GALDECO`, the old decorations painted): flint nodules along the beds of chalk, bedding in sandstone and clay, feldspar flecks in granite, cleavage in slate, stalactites that drip in limestone, wax and gut wall, a crust on salt, icicles, coal seams, lava cracks that pulse, a sheen on obsidian and glass, ribs in bone, crystal shards from roof and floor, coral knobs, pressed pages, rust streaks, shells, the rings of petrified wood, cogs that turn, stars that come and go, roots, glowing mushrooms.
- **What stands in a mine of that name** (`things`), at the miner's scale (`T_GAL.u`, 32 px to the metre: he is drawn at twice the strip's size, so a barrel comes to his waist): kegs, jars and bottles in a cellar, bones, a skull, an urn and gravestones in a crypt, powder kegs in a magazine, a chest and ingots in a treasury, cut blocks, a sawhorse and a winch in a quarry, a pump at a well, and otherwise what a working has (a barrel, a crate, a coil, spare props, a canary's cage, an anvil); a pick or a shovel against the wall; the level's number on a board. The tub on the rail is the island's own cart (`cartSprite`), and what has been found so far lies in it in its own colours.
- **The working face shows the same rock** (`mineFaceTex`): the tiles of the face canvas were flat squares with a speckle; they are the island's rock now, its beds running on from tile to tile, at half strength and half way to the kind's own wall colour so the tells still read (checked on a seeded face: the glints, the gas haze and the damp sheen stand out as before).
- **On a wide screen the miner stands clear of the panel** (`galLampX`): he stood at 36% of the scene, which on a desktop is behind the panel in the middle of it; there he stands just left of it.
- **What it costs.** A gallery takes 19 ms to paint at a phone's size and 29 at a desktop's (the slowest 79), once a level; a frame of the mine costs 0.13 to 0.18 ms, about half what it did (`cost.js`). `mineall.js` paints all 289 islands at levels 1, 4 and 8 at both sizes with the face's texture and must report no error; `mineshot.js ids|tier:t-a-b tag [WxH] [level]` puts mine screens side by side; `minetest.js island` plays a descent through (strike, go down twice, strike, the lamp, the climb out). The source is `land/mineart.js`, spliced with the rest.

## Every island its own sky (the fourteenth pass)

The landscape passes left the upper half of the background alone: over every island hung the same seven flat clouds (five rectangles each, at the same heights, drifting the same way), an overcast was seven grey rectangles, a storm island's sky five dark bars and the cloud an island hangs from a row of white columns. The sky is the island's own now.

- **A sky is strips of cloud painted once and drifted across** (`land/sky.js`; `CloudB`, `skyScape`, `T_SKY`): two an island, `hi` (the high cloud: mares' tails, mackerel fields, streaks, lenses) and `lo` (the heaps, towers and banks), each `T_SKY.wrap` (160 px) wider than the screen and coming round again, the high one at `speed.hi` (0.4 px a second) and the low at `speed.lo` (1.0), times the island's own `wind` (which has a direction: on some islands the clouds go the other way, and where it is 0 nothing moves). A strip is a buffer of tones, not colours (1 deep shade, 2 shade, 3 body, 4 lit, 5 rim, 6 and 7 veils that are half and a quarter there), so it is coloured again whenever the light changes (`skyStrip`, keyed by the hour's twelfth and the weather; about a millisecond).
- **Clouds have volume.** `CloudB.puffs` takes a heap of spheres and gives each pixel to the one nearest the eye (each sphere is asked only about its own square, into a small depth buffer: the first version asked every sphere about every pixel and a wall of towers took a tenth of a second on a slow phone); its normal is lit from the upper right like everything else in the game, the flat underside is in its own shadow, and the five tones are dithered into each other. The kinds of cloud (`SKYGEN`): `cumulus` (flat underneath, cauliflower on top), `tower` (lobes on lobes, each sunk into the one below and a little smaller, with heaps at its foot and sometimes an anvil), `bank` (long, low, ragged, with breaks), `mackerel` (small puffs in ranks inside a lens), `streak`, `wisp` (a mare's tail hooked up at the windward end), `lens` (stacked, smooth, the kind that stands over high ground); `virga` hangs rain that does not reach the sea under any of the heaped ones.
- **The kinds of sky** (`SKYKIND`: how a sky lays its clouds out; counts go with the strip's width, so a desktop's sky is as full as a phone's): `clear` (one small cloud), `fair`, `heaped` (two or three great heaps), `trade` (towers standing along the horizon over a warm sea), `wall` (a wall of them all round and blue overhead: the storm's eye, the monsoon), `mackerel`, `streaky`, `wisps`, `veil` (thin sheets: a milky sky), `banked` (a bank on the horizon, flat grey heaps over it), `lens`, `smoke`, `rows` (cloud streets in perspective: small and low far off, large and high overhead), `grid` (every cloud the same and in its place), `threads` (level fibres from side to side), `flecks` (a fleece), `none`. `fair` and `heaped` add the far ones (`K.far`): small flat heaps low over the sea, which is most of what gives the sky its depth.
- **A cloud takes the hour's light** (`skyPal`): its body from the sky's own lower colour, its shade toward the upper, the lit side white by day, warm for about two hours round sunrise and sunset (`T_SKY.low`, `warm`), moonlit slate by night; from 60% of the way down a cloud fades into the horizon's colour (`T_SKY.haze`), so a tower on the horizon is paler than a heap overhead; under an overcast, a rain or a storm the fair-weather cloud goes grey with it and loses its lit side (`skyGrey`, which fades with the weather).
- **The weather is painted too** (`skyDeck`, `skyFarDeck`, `skyWx`, `DECK_PAL`, `deckPal`): an overcast, a rain or a storm is a deck from the top of the picture down to a ragged base `T_SKY.deck` (0.38) of the way to the horizon (the HUD covers the top of the sky), its body in long soft billows, its underside in two ranks of bank with rags hanging from it, drawn over the scene by `drawWx` where the seven rectangles were (so the tops of mountains and of redwoods are in the cloud); and behind the land, drawn with the island's own clouds, ranks of the same weather going away under it to the horizon, each thinner and more broken than the last and the last only lines, with a bright gap along the sea. The first version stopped at the deck and left a plain blue sky under it; the ranks are what make it an overcast. Both fade in and out with the weather (`skyWx` gives the decks in the sky now and how far each has faded in). The deck goes over with the island's wind, a storm's fast (`speed.deck`, `speed.storm`), and where the island has none it stands. **Fog is painted too** (`skyFog`, `drawSkyFog`): its banks were nine pale rectangles; they are long banks lying on the water and a little above it, thick in the middle and thinning to nothing at their ends, with thinner veils over them, drifting on the island's wind over the same pale wash. And the rain leans the way the island's clouds go (`skyDir`). The four islands whose storm never lifts wear the storm deck always (the Standing Bolt's stands still with its lightning), and the six that live under or hang from a cloud have a white one at `T_SKY.ceiling` (0.2): the Hanging Isle's rocks hang from it now.
- **Every island's sky is written: all two hundred and eighty-nine and the spaceport** (`SKYS[id]` through `skySay`, `specs14a.js` to `specs14c.js`; an island with nothing said would still get a kind by its climate and seed: `skyOf`, `SKY_BY`). A line says the kind (`k`, and `k2` a second laid over it), how much cloud (`d`), how big (`z`), the `wind`, a `lift` up or down the sky, and where the island wants them a `tint` ([colour, how much]), `warm` (the low sun's light at any hour), `dark`, `virga`, `echo` (every cloud again beside itself, fainter), `flip` (the strips upside down: flat on top, heaped underneath, lit from below) and `add` (single clouds put where it wants them: [kind, along, down, width, strip, options]). The real islands have the skies of their seas: fair-weather heaps over Gull Harbor, one small cloud over the salt pans, towers along the horizon off the shoal and the spice port, wind-streaks over Gannet Rock and the albatross rock, a fleece over the shearing island and down over the eider farm, a Dutch sky over the windmill polder, the steppe's in ranks to the horizon, the monsoon standing on the delta's, low grey sitting on the iguana islands, lenses over the fjord and the reed lake, veils over the pack ice, kiln, kelp and smokehouse smoke where those are burned, purple in the dyers' cloud and pink in the flamingos'. From tier 5 the sky is the island's idea again: black clouds that drip over the Blot, amber ones over the Honey Shallows, five lines across the sky with notes on them over the Chord (and not moving over the frozen orchestra), a ruled grid over the chessboard, the Atlas and the island where everything is counted, every cloud twice over Echo Sound, the mirage flats and the island of two moons, clouds the wrong way up over Underheaven and the uphill tide, a sky in scales over the Changed Atoll and the serpent's coil, threads over the Loom and the great net and strings under the puppets' cloud, hooks all over the sky above the god's fishhook, one cloud for the island of one tree, none at all over Nameless, the first light always on Dawn Isle's and the last on Sundown Deep's, clouds that have not moved over the Held Breath, the stopped tide and the monastery of silence, and the wall of the storm going round the Eye.
- **The night is the island's own too, and the moon goes through its month** (`skyStars`, `drawSkyStars`, `SKY_STARS`, `moonC`, `moonPhase`, `drawSkyMoon`, `drawSkyBirds`; `skyAlso` in `specs14d.js` says more of a sky already written). The same seventy stars hung in the same places over every island. An island has its own field now, painted once: `T_SKY.stars` (2.2) to a thousand square pixels of sky, so a desktop's wider sky is as full as a phone's, times how clear its nights are: by its kind of sky (`SKY_STARS`: 1.5 under a clear one, 0.55 under veils, 0.45 under smoke) or what its line says (`stars`: 2.6 over the well of stars, 1.9 over the steppe and the dry isle, 0.85 over Gull Harbor's lamps, 0.3 in the fog, none at all over Nameless). Three brightnesses, a tenth warm and a tenth blue, the brightest with a glint, dimmer toward the horizon; a few twinkle and only those are drawn live. **From 1.25 (`T_SKY.milky`) the river of stars shows** (51 islands): a band across the sky at the island's own angle, with a dark lane down it, more faint stars than can be counted and a glow behind them. `stargrid` puts every star in its square (the island where everything is counted, the chessboard, the Atlas, Cogwater). They fade in with the night and go out under weather. **The moon** was a grey disc with a bite of sky painted over it, the same crescent every night; it is a disc with its seas now, in one of eight phases (`T_SKY.month`) that go round with the game's days (a month is 3.2 real hours), its dark side only earthshine, a halo by how full it is, and nothing at all at the new moon, when the stars have the sky. The path of light on the sea is pale under it and only as bright as the moon is full (`lightK`); the sun reddens as it nears the sea. It is for looks only: nothing bites by the moon (seasons and the moon as bite gates are still open). **Birds**: the same two gulls crossed every sky at the same heights; an island has its own count now (`birds`: one to three by its seed, four or five where the birds are the island: the gannet rock, the egg cliff, the albatross rock, the terns' strand, Gull Harbor; none where nothing lives: the dead sea, the monastery of silence, the Blot, the held breath), each bird its own way, height and speed.
- **Where it is drawn.** `drawSkyBase(h, t, { l })` draws the clouds of the island whose sky it is (the shore, the strip, the hunt, casting off and landfall, the storm's beach); with no island it is the open sea's fair sky (the title, the drift, the drowned morning, the passage, where `o.cloudT` runs the clouds' own clock fast so they go over as the days do: `T_SAIL.clouds`). A cavern has no sky. `sceneWeather` draws the fixed decks, `drawWx` the weather's. The old `CLOUDS` table and every rectangle cloud are gone.
- **What it costs.** A sky takes 2 to 3 ms to paint under a ×4 CPU throttle and 40 at most, once an island and screen size (five kept: `T_SKY.keep`; a weather's coloured strips are let go two minutes after it has gone, `T_SKY.idle`, so a long day on one island does not pile them up), a deck with its far ranks 17 to 24 when the weather first needs one, a strip about a millisecond to colour; a frame costs what it did (the shore 0.42 ms, the strip 0.37 to 0.49: four blits where there were forty rectangles). `skyall.js` paints all 292 skies (every island, the prologue's sea and the open sea) at a phone's size and a desktop's with every deck in every light, its stars and the eight moons, and must report no error and none empty (`THROTTLE=4` for the slow phone's times); `skyshot.js "island[:kind][@hour],…" tag [WxH] [zoom] [cols]` puts skies side by side with the HUD hidden (`WX=rain` for a sky of the hour, `DAY=3` for another night of the moon's month, `PRE=snippet.js` to try lines or a weather change without rebuilding) and `skymont.py` lays them out; `evtext.js snippet.js` runs a snippet in the game's own scope and prints what it returns.

## Performance (the lag pass)

Asked for after the game had become "extremely laggy". Profiled headlessly (Playwright with CDP CPU throttling ×4, a per-frame timer and canvas-call counters injected into a copy of the page); four causes, all fixed, none of them game logic (`pool`, `rollCatch`, `render`, `tick` and `saveNow` are each under 1.5 ms on a long voyage's save):

- **The index built every entry at once.** 6,536 rows (2,584 fish, 2,712 creatures, 1,240 minerals), each with its own canvas and a freshly generated sprite: 39,000 DOM nodes and 6,500 canvases, about five seconds on a desktop and far longer on a phone, and all of it stayed in the page after the sheet closed. Now the index shows **one record at a time** (tabs *Fish · Game · Minerals*, each with its count; `ixTab`), **filtered** (*This island* by default, *Caught*, *Everything*; `ixFilter`, `indexItems`: a fish is here if it has a home here or sits in one of the island's spots), **a screenful at a time** (`T.indexPage` 40 rows; `indexMore` appends the next forty and draws only their sprites when the sheet is scrolled near its end, with a *Show more* button as the fallback). A fish of many islands lists three and *and N more islands* (`homesText`). First open went from 21 s to 0.3 s under the ×4 throttle. **`closeSheet` empties the sheet** (`#sheet.innerHTML = ''`), so a closed sheet leaves nothing behind; every sheet is rebuilt by `renderSheet` when opened anyway. Never build a list of the whole world in one go again: page it.
- **The chart's fog was every pixel against every island.** `fogLayer` now stamps each island's circle and each sailed route into a `Float32Array` over its own bounding box only, then turns that into the dithered fog in one pass: pixel-identical (checked by hashing the layer for three sets of islands), 3.1 s → 59 ms with the whole sea known, 0.9 s → 23 ms with a third of it. It is still cached by the visited and known sets.
- **Scenery was redrawn rectangle by rectangle on every frame.** The strip's land (2,000–4,800 `fillRect` a frame), a `cliffs` hunting ground (6,600) and a mine's gallery (1,500–3,500) are static but for their own small animations. `layer(name, w, h, key, draw)` draws such a thing into an offscreen canvas by pointing the global `g` at it (so `g` is a `let` now, and every drawing function works unchanged), redraws it only every `T.sceneryMs` (125 ms, eight times a second: lamps, smoke and surf still move) or at once when `key` changes (the island, the ground line, a walk-in), and the frame blits it. Four layers: `strip` (the whole strip in world coordinates, `drawLand` + `drawPit` + `drawWild`, blitted under the camera's translate so walking scrolls it for nothing), `hills` (the far hills, keyed by the camera so it is redrawn only while walking), `hunt` (the island's painted hunting ground and its emitters, `drawHuntGround`) and `mine` (the island's painted gallery at this level and what moves in it: `drawMineArt`; keyed by level). The walker, animals, the shot, fish, floats, particles (`sceneFx`), the sky's weather, the miner, his lamp and the glints stay at full rate. The picture is the same to the pixel (checked old against new with the clock frozen, on a phone, a phone held sideways and a desktop: every strip, hunt and mine scene differs by 0 pixels). Script time under the ×4 throttle: the cliff strip 42% → 17% of a core, the cliff hunt 37% → 15%, an ordinary strip 24% → 14%, the mine 20% → 6%. **New scenery that does not move every frame goes in a layer**; anything that must be smooth is drawn after the blit.
- **Small things.** `renderIsle` ran on every frame of a walk and rewrote the panel's HTML each time; it now builds a key of what it shows (money, clock, the prompt, the hint, the beacon) and leaves the page alone when nothing changed (`isleUI`). `skyLayer` and `seaLayer`, rebuilt every five seconds, worked out a colour per pixel; they now work out the eleven band colours once.
- To measure again: the scratchpad's `prof.js` (frame times and the top self-time functions per scene under a CPU throttle), `cost.js` (script ms and canvas calls per frame per scene), `sheets.js` (how long each sheet takes to open, and how many DOM nodes and canvases the page holds after), `vis.js` (old build against new, pixel by pixel, with `Date.now` frozen). They run against a copy of the page with `window.__G` exported and a timer wrapped round `frame` (`mk.py`).

## Catch screen and control panel (third pass)

- **Every landing opens a catch screen.** Headline by priority (Legendary! → Gigantic! → Albino! → Trophy! → New record! → New species! → Landed!), a rarity line under the name in the tier's colour (with the variant and its worth multiplier beside it), tag pills for the rest, the fish lit in a dark panel, name, size, a sub line (rig, time since the line went out), and three stat tiles: worth, bait, time of day. Driftwood and junk get the same screen with a single button. The size line shows length and weight; weight comes from `kgOf` (a condition factor per body shape in `K_SHAPE`, overridable per species with `k`, times length cubed, with ±8% jitter rolled at catch time and stored as `kg`). The index keeps `bestKg` beside `best`; the inventory lists both.
- **The fish is held until you decide.** At a market: **Sell** (that one fish, money now) or **Keep** (bucket, sell the haul later). Elsewhere: **Keep** or **Release**. **Bucket full: the inventory opens on its own over the card** (the sheet stacks above the card, `z-index` 21; once per landed fish, `roomAskedFor`) with a red line naming the fish that is waiting and the bucket listed cheapest first (only then; the plain inventory keeps catch order); **Let go** on any fish frees a slot and hands you straight back to the card with Keep lit (`releaseFish`), as does Sell the haul at a market (`sellHaul`); close the sheet instead and the card offers Release, with a **Make room** button that reopens the inventory. Tapping outside the card keeps the fish (or releases it if the bucket is full). The pending catch lives in `S.pending` and is saved, so a reload reopens the screen. The index is updated at landing time, so a released fish still counts as caught.
- **The panel is the always-there tab.** Tide line; `LINES` header with a live "n lines ready" / "n on!" count; four line cards (a locked card says "shop", never a price: lines are bought only at a shop counter; a card with a fish on it, rod HIT! or passive BITE!, turns red and pulses a glow); a one-line summary of the selected line's rig, bait, rod and line test with a single **CHANGE RIG** button that opens the **rig sheet** (this line's rig and bait as segmented pickers, then every rod, line and bucket you own with the one in use marked and a USE button on the rest, then lines, weapon and boat for reference), every entry with its **gear icon**; **the pickers list only what you have ever had, plus one empty slot** (a dashed box labelled *empty*) while anything is still unobtained: there are too many baits, rods and shot to list them all, so the slot stands for whatever you equip later (`S.seen` records every bait, shot, call and rig ever bought, derived from what a save holds on load; the rod, set line, scraps, stones and no-call are always known, and the notes under the pickers hint without naming; `icon(key, true)` still draws a dark silhouette if one is ever wanted); nothing unfolds inline any more; the rig note; the big Cast / Set the line / Pull in / Reel in / Haul in button (dark with a brass border, `.cta.go`, so it sits with the panel instead of shouting; only Reel in! on a hit is loud); then a bottom bar with **Inventory** (bucket count and value) and, at a market, **Sell haul**. The driftwood row stays above the bar on the shoal before the raft.
- **Inventory sheet.** Bucket list with sprites, size, trophy star, value and a Let go button per fish; whole-bucket total and Sell the haul at a market; bait counts; gear (rod, line, bucket, boat, weapon, lines, trophy rig, driftwood), each with its icon.
- **Every piece of gear has a pixel icon.** `ICON_MASKS` are 20×20 letter masks (a main, b dark, c accent, d highlight, e extra): a rod, a line spool, five buckets, three baits, the set-line stake and the trophy buoy, the weapons (sling, bow, crossbow, spear, blowgun, gun, throwing stick), shot, a call, a cloak, boots, a pack, a boat. `ICON_PAL` maps an item key (`rod:cane`, `line:l80`, `pail:chest`, `bait:squid`, `rig:set`, `weapon:bow`, `boat:skiff`…) to a mask and its colours, so the five rods share one drawing with five palettes and the eight lines are one spool with eight line colours. `iconC` caches a canvas per key and scale, `icon(key)` emits the `<canvas class="icon">`, `drawIcons(el)` fills them after a sheet renders. They show in the rig sheet (pickers and gear rows), the shop (every item) and the inventory's gear list, so a thing looks the same wherever it appears.
- **Every species has its own sprite, built from a body plan.** `FISH_ART` gives each species a plan (`gen`: size, body depth, where the body is deepest, peduncle and snout thickness, tail type `fork`/`lunate`/`hetero`/`round`/`trunc`/`none`, dorsal and anal fins by type and span, adipose fin, pelvic fin, barbels, a beak, an underbite, open `jaws` with teeth, an angler's lure, a tail light) or a hand-drawn silhouette (`sil`, used for the sunfish), plus a palette (`col`: a saturated body colour, a bright belly, fin, mark, glow) and a pattern (`pat`: bars, saddles, stripes, lateral line, spots, scales, lights, bands, scutes…). `genSil` rasterises the silhouette on a padded canvas and trims it; `shadeSil` adds the outline, sheen, back/mid/belly tones, fin rays, the eye and the pattern. `fishPal` derives the shades the way hand-pixelled fish are coloured: shadows darker and shifted toward blue, the highlight lighter and warmer, the outline a deep tint of the body rather than black. Composition follows the pixel-fish idiom without copying any sprite: chunky proportions (bodies compressed toward about 2:1, deep-bodied fish nearly round), thick fins and tails, a big white eye (3×3 on deep bodies) with a glint on the head, and five tones of volume from back to belly: a bright rim under the outline, a dark band, body colour, a lit lower band, then the belly. Patterns stay off the rim so the volume reads. That five-step gradient is what makes the sprites look rounded rather than flat. Sprites run 24–54 px wide and 11–28 tall, two to four times the pixels of a 16 px fish, in line with the "higher resolution than Stardew" decision. They appear on the catch screen (always 3×, so size differences show), in the index (unknown species as a dark silhouette), in the inventory and in the fight. The swimming fish in the scene stay one-colour silhouettes on purpose: they are far off and under murky water. The fisherman and the scenes have not had their real art pass yet.

`MODE` is `title` / `intro` (the card before the prologue) / `cut` / `sail` (the three-beat voyage between islands) / `game` (the fishing shore) / `island` (the strip) / `hunt` / `mine`. Sailing lands you on the fishing shore (or the spaceport's apron). Passive lines keep resolving in every mode but title and cut.

Line state machine: `idle → waiting (rod: 6–16 s, 3–7 s in the prologue) → bite (rod, 4–10 s window by difficulty) → fight → idle` or `idle → waiting → hooked (set/trophy, waits forever) → haul or fight → idle`. The hit window stops counting once the fight starts (the line stays in `bite` while `F` is live, and `tick` leaves it alone), and Reel in / Haul in do nothing during a fight. One rod can be active at a time; set lines can all be out at once.

Catch value: `valueOf(sp, f, trophy, jit)` = the species' worth by rarity (`T_VALUE`, `worthTable`) × 0.7–1.4 by where the fish sits in its length range × (1 ± 0.15), ×1.5 at trophy size (size fraction ≥ 0.85), floor $3. Weight comes from `kgOf` and is for the line, the card and the record, not the price. Trophy line skews size up (`u^0.45`), set line skews down and caps at 0.8 on a quick hook, rod is linear; a long soak lifts either passive line toward the top of the range.

## How to run and test

- **It is a website, for phone and computer.** https://zik3434.github.io/Castaway/ is the game; `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes the repository root to GitHub Pages on every push to `main` (and to the working branch), with nothing built. `manifest.webmanifest` and the `icons/` folder make it installable to a phone's home screen (full screen, own icon), and `sw.js` is a small service worker: network first for the page with `cache: 'no-cache'` (GitHub Pages sends a ten-minute `max-age`, and without it the browser's HTTP cache hid a fresh push) so a push lands on the next load, cache first for icons and fonts, so the game opens with no signal; it is registered with `updateViaCache: 'none'`. It registers only over http(s), never from `file://`. Bump `VERSION` in `sw.js` when the cached shell list changes. The desktop gets keyboard play: Space or Enter presses the selected line's big button (cast, reel, haul, pull in), Space holds the net in the fight, Escape closes a sheet or the catch card, and the arrow keys walk the island strip. The layout is the same on both: the scene fills the window at an integer scale and the panel stays 520 px wide in the middle.
- Open `index.html` in any browser, or serve the folder with any static server. Fonts load from Google Fonts; everything else is inline. Works offline except the fonts (monospace fallback) on `file://`, and fully offline once the service worker has run.
- Reset a save: there is no wipe any more; start a new voyage under a new code (the old one stays under its code). In the browser console `localStorage.clear()` and reload forgets every code on that device (the cloud keeps them).
- Cloud saves: set `SYNC.url` to the Firebase Realtime Database URL and paste the rules from the voyage-code bullet above into the database's Rules tab. To test without Firebase, run `node mockdb.js` (scratchpad) and seed `localStorage['castaway-sync-url'] = 'http://localhost:8899'` in the Playwright context; `codes.js` drives two devices through it.
- Visual check without a phone: Playwright with a 390×760 mobile viewport, screenshot the title, each cutscene beat (click `#cut` to advance) and the game. Animated buttons (`.cta.hit`) need `{ force: true }` to click in Playwright.
- To test the island loop fast, lower `T.setMean` or raise the `wood` weight in `SPECIES`.
- **A `//` comment in the middle of a one-line statement comments out the rest of the line, silently.** Most of this file is long lines, and a note dropped between two statements on one of them takes everything after it: the taken card's strip of ground had not been drawn since the eleventh pass (`x.fillStyle = …; // the island's own ground under it x.fillRect(…); …`), and the bathhouse island's steam had never risen from its middle layer (`// a boardwalk of them, out over the hot river steam(M, …);`). Both were found by reading, months of commits later; `node --check` cannot see it. Put a note at the end of the line or in `/* … */`, and after a pass scan the script for an end-of-line comment whose text looks like code (the scratchpad's `comscan.py`: a `//` after code whose tail holds `name(…);` or an assignment, or ends like a statement; last run: none left).
- **A function name declared twice is not an error, and the later one wins everywhere** (it broke the icehouse's fog for a whole pass and two landscapes for an hour: see the bank section). After adding helpers, scan the extracted script: every `^function name(` must be unique (`node -e` over the scratchpad's `game.js`, a dozen lines; it prints the duplicates). `landall.js` (every island's landscape built in the fishing view, the strip and the hunt, console errors and warnings collected) is the other half of that check and must report none, and so must `wildall.js` (every island's gate screen, mine entrance and hunting ground, at a phone's width and a desktop's) `spotall.js` (every fishing spot's water, 1,446 of them with the offshore ones, at both sizes), `mineall.js` (every island's gallery at three levels, at both sizes) `farall.js` (every island's skyline as the passage shows it, at both sizes) and `skyall.js` (every island's sky, with every weather's deck in every light, at both sizes).
- To check the catch tables without a browser: extract the script and run `pool()` for every location × phase × bait × rig × every sky the island's climate gives; none should be empty (last run, with 2,584 fish across 289 fishing islands: 222,592 spot pools, none empty, every sky-only fish reachable; no spot is empty even on 10 lb line with scraps by day, and the variety diagnostic beside it counts at least four resident species in every spot's set-line pool on scraps and 10 lb at every hour, the moth island's night water apart; every hunting ground has sling-sized game at every phase; the spaceport is skipped). The reach check lives beside it: with the boats' reaches every island of tier t is reachable on boat t, hopping from the shoal. The mine check beside those: every island has a mine of a known kind, a maul-soft mineral at level 1, a direct pool at levels 1 to 3 and exactly one relic with lore (last run: 289 mines, 1240 minerals, zero problems; the lowest maul-soft share of a level-1 pool is 14%).

## Art direction

- Side-on cross-section filling the screen: sky, surface line, underwater, with the shore on the left third and open water to the right. The fisherman sits at the water's edge on something that belongs to the location (wreck plank, pier end, rock, his own deck, ice). He is never seated on air: where the bank has an edge (a quay, a staging, a ledge, a ship's side) he sits on it with his legs over, on a flight of steps he sits on a step, and anywhere else he sits on the island's own `SEAT` with his feet on the ground (a creel, a salt sack, a block of ice, a whale's vertebra); boats get a thwart and the raft a crate (`crateSeat`). Lines go out to floats spread across the water. **Every set and trophy line is its own rod on a stand** (`drawRodStand`): a forked stake on the ground to his left (on the boat's deck out on the water), a rod resting in the fork with its butt behind and its tip out over the water, the line running from the tip to the float; a trophy rod is darker and heavier with a red reel at the butt, an idle stand shows the rod with the hook hanging from the tip, and when a fish is on the tip is hauled down in jerks and the stand rattles, while the rod in his hands stays the hand line's. The stake the passive lines once ran from is gone.
- Palette: tar navy ground, brass accents, lantern light. Day cycle through dawn/day/dusk/night sky keyframes; the sun rises over the sea and sets behind the island.
- Each island needs its own shore, seabed, landscape, village building, hunting ground, fish roster, creatures, keeper lines and legend. The data-driven `scene` + `village` + `hunt` descriptors and the prop and building tables exist so that a new island is mostly data; every shore is a painted bank (`BANK[id]`). Budget content, not code: an island is only worth adding when it feels nothing like the one before.
- Replace the string-mask sprites with real sprite sheets once the resolution is decided. Keep `image-rendering: pixelated` and integer scaling. Keep the caching pattern: anything static per hour or per location is rendered once to an offscreen canvas, and scenery with only small animations of its own goes through `layer` (see the performance section).

## Conventions for working in this repo

- Keep it dependency-free and single-file until the roadmap says otherwise. No frameworks, no bundler.
- Every wait, price and weight is data in the tables at the top; change numbers there, not in logic.
- Never add art, names, sounds or UI copied from existing games.
- When adding an island, prefer writing it as a compact block (scene, village, hunt, keeper, spots, fish, legend, creatures) and emitting the entries, the way the hundred and ten newer islands were made; the emitted entries are the source of truth afterwards. Every island with a keeper (shops and traders alike) gets a `FACES` entry that looks like its island and like nobody else's, every island gets an `ARRIVE` entry (what it does as you pull in), and every island gets a `mine` (a kind from `MINE_KIND`, a name, a gate line) with four to seven minerals in `MINERALS` (real ones from the catalogue through tier 4, its own invented stone beyond; at least one the maul can pry at level 1, a direct pool at levels 1 to 3, a rare or two in the deep) and a relic with a line of lore in `RELIC_LORE`. Check the chart: islands need about 17 px between centres on the 400×300 world, and the sandbar list in `worldLayer` must not sit under one; when the sea is full at that spacing, grow `MW`/`MH` (and move The Stars' glyph to the new corner) rather than crowd it.
- When adding an island: an entry in `LOCS` with links both ways, a `scene` (or a hand-drawn shore), a `village`, a `hunt`, 3–5 `spots` each with a known-for line and a roster (every island fish in at least one spot, commons in several, the legend in one), a keeper with three lines and a rumor, 5–7 fish with sprites (real for tiers 1–4, invented from tier 5), 2–4 creatures (a silhouette from `BEAST_SIL` and a palette), and a legend with lore; then its own landscape (`LANDS[id]`: what the island is known for where the eye can see it from the water), its own village building (`SHOP[id]` through `shopSay`: a form, its materials, a hanging sign, two or three things at the door) and its own bank (`BANK[id]` through `bankSay`: a form, what it is made of, a seat, and four or five things with the one a phone will show first), and its own sky (`SKYS[id]` through `skySay`: the kind that belongs over it, how much, how big, the wind, and a tint or a twist if the island is one of the unreal ones), each looked at on a contact sheet beside its neighbours so it resembles none of them. Then the pool check: every spot × phase × bait × rig non-empty, and every hunting ground at every phase; and `landall.js`, which must report no island without a landscape, no error and no warning (a thing, a strew or an edge named in a bank that does not exist is a warning).
- Rosters are deep (10 to 40 fish per island, every spot at least four scrap-bait fish at every hour); prefer new islands over more fish on existing ones, and when a spot feels thin, check its set-line pool on scraps and 10 lb by hour before adding anything (the scratchpad's variety diagnostic does it for every island). The generated species sit in `SPECIES` and `FISH_ART` as plain entries like every other fish; there is no generator in the repo.
- Rarity is derived, not set: a new species gets its tier from its island weight, the bait it needs and its `rare` flag unless it pins `tier`. Worth is derived too (`T_VALUE`): a new entry's `rate` only decides where it stands among the others of its tier and rarity, so give it a rate like its peers' and never try to price a thing by hand. Variant palettes are generated from the base palette (`variantPal`), so a new species needs no extra art for albino or mutated.
- When adding a mineral: a real one belongs to its island's geology (chalk and flint on a chalk island, cassiterite where there is tin, fossils in sedimentary rock, native metals and gems in the deep); give it a `loc` weight (22+ common, 12+ uncommon, 7+ rare, under 7 epic), a Mohs `hard`, a specimen `kg` range (grams for gems and nuggets), a `rate` like its peers' (worth comes from rarity and tier: `T_VALUE`), a `depth` window (soft commons at 1–3, the precious at 4–8), a silhouette from `MINERAL_SIL` and a palette (`base` and any of `light`, `dark`, `acc`, `mark`, `glow`); the pattern defaults by kind (facets for gems and crystals, flecks for ores and metals) and can be pinned with `pat`. A real mineral that already exists gets a second `loc` on the existing entry, never a copy. Judge sprites on a contact sheet first.
- When adding a creature: pick a silhouette from `BEAST_SIL` (twenty-eight: a wolf, a cat, a goat, a monkey, an ox, a rat, an owl, a horse, a scorpion, a camel, an elephant and a penguin beside the first sixteen; or draw a new one: `#` body, `e` eye, `l` legs, `d` feet, `a` accent, `w` white, `m` mark) and a palette; the shading is automatic. Exotic islands should get shapes that are theirs, not a recoloured hare.
- When adding a species: pick a real fish that belongs in that location's biome (invented only on exotic islands); add location weight, time window, real size range, value, behavior, difficulty, min bait, rare flag, scene `shape`, a sky it minds if it should (`weather` to gate it, `likes` to tilt it; gate only a fish whose spots keep another windowless scrap-bait fish), and a `FISH_ART` entry (a body plan and palette; copy the closest existing plan and adjust, or a hand-drawn `sil` for odd shapes). Then re-run the empty-pool check. Judge sprites on a contact sheet first: render every `fishMap` at 3× in a scratch page.
- Fish are drawn head right, tail left; the scene flips them as needed.
- Save format is versioned (`v: 18`). Bump it and write a migration in `load()` when the shape changes.
- Testing in Playwright: the game saves on `visibilitychange`, so a reload overwrites anything you put in `localStorage` from the page. Seed a save with `context.addInitScript` in a fresh context instead.
- Reference material the designer liked: One Cast (onecast.github.io) for the fast-travel, multi-line, location-and-bite-window structure; Stardew Valley for the fight mechanic. Reference only.

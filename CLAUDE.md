# Castaway

A pixel-art fishing, hunting and mining game. You are a fisherman stranded on an island. You fish your way off it, then fish, hunt and dig your way across a fictional sea of wildly different islands, buying rods, lines, bait, weapons, picks, lamps and boats, and (around 200 hours in) a starship, to fish in space. Travel is by chart; on each island you walk a short strip between the fishing spot, the village and the hunting ground.

`index.html` is the entire playable prototype (vanilla JS, canvas, no build step). It is published as a website at https://zik3434.github.io/Castaway/ (GitHub Pages, deployed by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` from the repository root on every push to `main`); open it there or open the file in a browser. Read this file before changing anything; it holds every design decision made so far, what is placeholder, and what comes next.

## Decisions already made (do not relitigate without asking)

- **Stranded is the hook, and it is used.** You start on an island with no market, with one rod and one line (a second line costs $60 at the first port; `SLOT_PRICES`). Fish go in a bucket. Driftwood comes up on the lines; six pieces build a raft. The raft is the first boat. The first port buys the whole haul, and that is the moment money starts to exist. Selling is irrelevant until you leave the island.
- **Gear is four ladders, and each does one thing.** The **rod** widens the net in the fight and carries `luck`, which tilts every rod cast toward rarer fish (weight × (1 + rank × luck × 0.5), rank 0 common to 3 epic, 1 for a legend); five rods from the stick to the star-iron. **Bait** gates species by tier and adds luck of its own (scraps 0, shrimp 0.3, squid 0.8). The **line** is a test weight (`LINE_TEST`: 10 lb at the start, then 20, 40, 80, 150, 300, 600 and a 2,000 lb cable): a species whose smallest adult outweighs the line never bites, and anything else is held to the line's weight (the catch card says so, with a LINE HELD pill; large and gigantic variants are lost when held). Legends need the line that takes their smallest, then run free. A cast with nothing in the pool is refused with a toast naming the line. The **bucket** is bought in sizes (`BUCKETS`: pail 10, basket 20, barrel 35, ice chest 60, hold 100); `bucketCap()` replaces the old constant. **Everything bought is kept**: `rodsOwned`, `linesOwned`, `pailsOwned` list every rung owned, `rod`/`line`/`pail` are the ones in use, the rig sheet swaps between them, and the shop sells the rung above the best owned. Saves are `v: 11`; a v9 save gets the line its rod implies and a basket, and v10 saves own every rung below what they hold, so nobody loses what they could already catch or carry.
- **Passive lines soak; they do not count down.** A set or trophy line has a small chance of a hook every second, so the wait is exponential and memoryless (`waitFor` samples it once at cast time from `T.setMean` / `T.trophyMean`, capped at `soakCap` means, so it still survives closing the game). The card shows time soaked, never time left. **The longer it soaks, the bigger the fish**: `soakBonus` is 0 at one mean and 1 at `soakGrow` means, and the size fraction climbs toward the top of the range with it, so a set line that sat a long while can land trophy size (about one hook in ten) while a quick hook is a small fish.
- **Passive lines bring volume and cash. Skill lines bring trophies.** A set line hooks common fish with no fight and only reaches trophy size after a long soak. The rod gives a bite you must answer within a few seconds, then a bar-and-fish fight; rare species come only from the rod or the trophy line.
- **The trophy line is the expensive passive line.** Long wait, needs better bait, hooks big fish, but you still have to land it in the fight when you come back. It exists so patience pays without replacing skill.
- **"Leave the rod out and wait" means during a session, while you work another line.** Passive lines also keep ticking while the game is closed: a hooked fish is saved as hooked and waits for you. Passive lines do not earn money offline on their own; you still have to land and sell.
- **The world is a branching chart of very different islands, revealed by neighbours and rumors.** Boat tiers: raft 1, skiff 2, cutter 3, trawler 4, clipper 5, ironclad 6, starship 7 (space). **Distance matters too**: every boat has a `reach` in chart pixels (raft 45, skiff 60, cutter 80, trawler 105, clipper 135, ironclad 175, starship anywhere; about 30 px is a day's sail), and a voyage needs both the island's tier and the island within reach of where you are (`withinReach`, `boatReach`, `sailDays`), so the farther islands of a tier wait for the next boat and you hop island to island. The chart draws a dotted brass ring of the current boat's reach around you, the legend says *N days out, too far for your raft* and dims those rows, and the sail button says why. A headless check hops the whole world: with these reaches every island of tier t is reachable on boat t (the minimum that still connects everything is 23 px now that the fourth expansion has filled the gaps); from the shoal the raft reaches eight of the fifteen tier-1 islands, Gull Harbor at the edge. Re-run that check when moving an island or changing a reach. Every tier has two islands and routes cross between them, so there are always several paths. An island is hidden until it is linked to somewhere you have been (then a fogged `???` you can still sail to if your boat reaches) or until a shopkeeper names it in a rumor (then its name and rumor line show, marked with a star). Shopkeepers from tier 3 on (Old Sajan at the delta, and everyone beyond) hear about the exotic islands first. Rod → bigger fish → money → boat → range → new islands → rarer fish is still the ladder.
- **Every island is its own culture, One Piece style, and they get more exotic with distance.** Tiers 1–4 are real places with real fish and animals: a salt-traders' cay, a lantern-lit river wharf, a monsoon delta on stilts, a drowned city's reef, a frozen fjord. Tier 5 on is exotic with invented fish and game: a volcano island whose fish swim in fire, an atoll of water like air, a lake inside a mountain, shoals under a storm that never moves. Space is tier 7. The current set is in `LOCS`; the bank of ideas for more is below.
- **Each island is a strip of four screens you walk:** the shaft head on the far left, then the hunting gate, the village, and the fishing shore on the right (the normal fishing scene); an island with no mine is three (`isleScreens`). Each has one thing to press: the shaft head down into the mine, the gate into the wild, the village building (the shop, the traders' tent, or your camp), and the fishing spot. The shop is no longer a tab; you walk to it. Walking is the only character movement in the game and it is deliberately short: `T.walkSpeed` (72 logical px/s, about eight seconds across the strip) and a four-frame walk cycle at `T.walkFps` (`walkA` contact with the back heel lifted and the arms swung, `stand` raised a pixel as the legs pass, the mirrored `walkC`, `stand` again; `isleFrame`). You leave the fishing shore for the strip with the green **EXIT** button at the far right of the shore's tab bar (`#tWalk`, `.tabs button.exit`); the strip's own tabs are Map, Index, Bag, Menu, and the Menu sheet also carries a Map button, so the chart is never more than two taps away.
- **Spots are kinds of water, not just docks.** Every spot has a `kind` (shore, pier, river, lake, reef, deep water, rocks, kelp, lagoon, cave, lava, hot spring, salt flat, ice, mangrove, weed, ruins, open sea; names in `KIND_NAME`), shown as a brass label in the Spots sheet. Every fishing island has a fifth spot of kind `open` with `boat: true`: open water fished from your own boat, locked until you have one (the raft counts), with the island's big travellers and rarities at higher weight and a few commons so set lines still hook. On the water the scene swaps the shore for your boat on deep water with the island a haze behind you (`drawOpenWater`).
- **Descriptions hint; they never list.** A spot's "known for" line, the tide line, a keeper's rumor and an island's blurb describe the water and what it feels like, not the roster: "something coils in the lantern light after dark", never "conger at night". The tide line (`waterText`) reads the pool's shape (a heavy share, a rarity, a legend in its window, a stray) into a line that rotates with the hour. The index and the catch card are where names live.
- **Every island has 3–5 named fishing spots, each known for something.** `spots` on an island: a `kind`, a name, a "known for" line, and a roster of species multipliers (`boat: true` on the open-water spot). Rosters overlap (the commons appear at several spots) but every spot has fish the others lack, and each legend lives at exactly one spot. The pool is spot-based: weight = the species' island weight × the spot multiplier, so a spot with no entry for a fish never gives it. Spots are picked from a sheet (the Spots tab on the fishing screen, or the fishing spot on the island strip); moving pulls in your lines. The current spot shows in the HUD clock line.
- **The chart is a map of the whole world, blacked out.** One sea with every island as a drawn landmass and a few uninhabited sandbars (400×300, `MW`/`MH`, grown from 240×180 with the fifth expansion and again from 300×220 with the sixth, each time the old sea was full at the 17 px spacing islands keep between centres; the new water lies east and south, and the spaceport moved to the new bottom-right corner) under near-black fog that clears in a wide circle around islands you have visited, a smaller one around islands a keeper named, a sliver around neighbouring land you have only heard of (the `???`), and along routes you have sailed between visited islands. Space (The Stars) is a glyph, not land. The fog is an ImageData layer cached by visited/known sets.
- **Creatures are drawn with the same volume as the fish, and so are the minerals** (see the mining bullets).
- **Creatures are drawn with the same volume as the fish.** `shadeBeast` lays five tones from the rim down (highlight, lit back, body, shaded flank, belly), puts the rear quarter in shadow and lifts the front as if lit from the upper right, flecks the body with fur, and drops a shadow where each leg meets the body. Sixteen silhouettes now: hare, fox, deer, boar, bird, crab, lizard, seal, bat, moth, frog, turtle, snake, bear, stork, croc. The exotic islands' game uses the shapes that fit them: pad frogs, moss turtles and a pad viper on the lily pads, glow frogs in the lantern bay, a salt serpent on the dead sea, a dream serpent on the sleeper, a polar bear on the berg, a cave bear in the mountain, crocodiles in the swamps, storks on the flats.
- **The stalk is ours.** A sighting starts far off and the band is narrow. You can **creep closer** up to three times; every step widens the band (×1.45 per step) and raises the animal's **alertness** by its skittishness × the **wind** (rolled per stalk and drawn as drifting seeds: in your face is cheap, across is normal, at your back costs nearly double) × 2 if the animal has its **head up** (it pauses to look every few seconds, marked on the scene and in red on the panel; creeping then is nearly always fatal). Alertness also climbs on its own and at full it bolts. A point-blank shot is worth more (+10% per step) and the taken card says the distance and the wind. All numbers in `T_HUNT`: the band is the weapon's `acc` × (`bandBase` 1.3 − the animal's diff), the mark swings with a period of `swing[0]` − `swing[1]` × diff seconds (1.8 − 1.0) and at `holdSlow` (0.45) of that while you hold, breath lasts `breath` (3.2 s), the temper climbs over `alertMs` (11 s less 5 s × diff) and a step costs `creepCost` (0.13 + 0.22 × diff) before wind, look and cloak; these were eased once from 1.15 / 1.5−0.95 / 0.55 / 2.6 s / 9−4.5 s / 0.16+0.24 when the first stalks felt too tight. Push your luck or take the long shot: that is the decision every stalk asks.
- **Hunting is the second loop.** Behind every island's gate is a hunting ground with its own creatures (real animals on real islands, invented ones beyond). **Creatures have a rarity like fish, shown in colour** (`gameRarityOf`: fabled for the once-ever ones; the `rare` flag is epic at diff 0.75+ or ground weight ≤ 3, else rare; otherwise by ground weight: 24+ common, 14+ uncommon, 8+ rare, under 8 epic) on the taken card under the name, in the creature index and in the game bag. **One fabled creature per island, taken once ever**: a hundred and fifty-eight of them (`fab_<island>` in `GAME`, `legend: true`, lore in `BEAST_LORE`), each a sliver of its ground's weight (0.4), one narrow hour, difficulty 0.88–0.95, and a smallest adult just above the weapon its island's tier hands you (tier 0 9 kg, 1 14, 2 32, 3 72, 4 165, 5 300, 6 680 kg) the way a legend fish needs the next line; worth scales from about $850 at the shoal to about $70,000 at tier 6. They have their own tier, **Fabled**, in ember (`RARITY.fabled`, `#ff7f50`) against the fish legends' gold; the card headlines FABLED! with the lore and *once ever*; once in `S.gindex` the creature leaves the ground's pool (`gamePool`). The Pale King on the shoal, Old Tallow at the harbor, Rime Mother in the fjord, The Emperor at the rookery, The Sand Turtle in the hourglass, The Tuskless Bull at the haulout, The Anchor Bear on the iron island. A stalk is wait → sighting → (creep) → draw → loose: a mark swings along a bar, you hold to draw (the mark slows, your breath runs out), and let go with the mark inside the band to take the animal. Band width is the weapon less the animal's skittishness; swing speed is the animal; wait too long and it bolts. Game goes in a bag and sells with the fish. Creatures have their own index.
- **Hunting gear is ladders too, and each does one thing** (mirroring the fishing gear). The **weapon** (`WEAPONS`, fourteen rungs from the sling to the star-iron bow by way of throwing stick, short bow, hunting bow, blowgun, atlatl, longbow, crossbow, recurve, heavy crossbow, harpoon gun, musket and rifle) sets the band (`acc`) and the heaviest game it brings down (`power`, kg): a creature whose smallest adult outweighs the weapon never shows itself, the way a light line is never bitten, so the sling (12 kg) sees hares and birds while the elephant seals wait for the harpoon gun; a `quiet` weapon makes every creep cheaper, a `loud` one (musket, rifle) scatters the ground so the next sign takes longer. **Shot** (`SHOT`, spent per shot, picked on the kit sheet like bait: stones free, flint heads and silver shot tilt the roll toward rarer game by `luck` and `gameRank`, barbed heads pay ×1.25, poison darts keep the animal calm) and **calls** (`CALLS`, spent per stalk: a bird call halves the wait, a rattle brings rarer game, musk starts the stalk a step nearer). **Cloaks** (`CLOAKS`, five, `calm` slows alertness from scent and sight), **boots** (`BOOTS`, four, more creeping steps before point blank; `maxSteps()` replaces the constant) and **game bags** (`PACKS`, five, 10 to 100; `packCap()`). Everything bought is kept (`weaponsOwned`, `cloaksOwned`, `bootsOwned`, `packsOwned`) and swapped on the **kit sheet** (`sheetKind === 'kit'`, from the hunting ground's Kit tab, its CHANGE KIT button or the rig sheet), every entry with a pixel icon. Shops stock the next rung of each ladder from the island tier in its `tier` field, and shot and calls by the pack. Every hunting ground has sling-sized game at every hour (checked headlessly), and the star-iron bow takes everything. Saves are `v: 13`; a v12 save owns every weapon below the one it carried.
- **Mining is the third loop, and it is a push-your-luck game about reading rock.** Behind every island's shaft head is a mine (`mine` on the island: a `kind` from `MINE_KIND`, a name, the gate line) with its own minerals: real ores, gems and fossils on the real islands (chalk, galena, cassiterite, ammonites, Baltic amber, a megalodon tooth), invented stone from tier 5 (ember glass, air glass, night pearls, pressed pages, swallowed gold). A descent (`MODE === 'mine'`, `MI`, saved in `S.mine` so a reload resumes it) is a **face** of six by three rock tiles drawn on a canvas (`drawMineFace`) over hidden pockets of mineral, gas and water. **Tap a tile to strike it** (one oil): it opens on rubble, on a stone, on gas (the lamp flares off a quarter of its oil) or on a spring (the tile and everything under it floods). **The tells are the game**: a rock tile shows a glint in the colour of each mineral beside it, a green haze beside gas, a damp sheen beside water, so you read the face before you spend oil on it; a dowsing twig makes every tell plain and two tiles out, a canary shows all the gas. **An opened stone is pried** with a raised pick: hold PRY and the mark climbs the meter over `T_MINE.pryRise` (1.5 s); let go inside the band the stone's hardness sets (`pryBand`: soft stone low on the meter, hard stone high, the band narrower the harder it is and wider for every point your pick has over it, `pryEase`); land within `shatterSlack` band-widths of it and the stone only chips (half worth, the **Chipped** variant), further and it shatters. A clean pry can be **Flawless** (×3, 3%) or a **Giant** (×2.5, 4%). **The roof tires with every tile broken** (`T_MINE.roof`, more per level) and at full it comes down and the descent is over; pit props halve it. **Deeper costs oil and so does the climb back** (`descend`, `climb`; level 1 is free to leave), minerals lie in depth windows (`depth`), deeper is richer and dearer, and the shaft goes to level 8; **the lamp is the whole budget** and if it dies on the ladder the heaviest find of the descent is dropped in the dark. Finds go in the sack and sell with the haul; the found card (`showFound`) mirrors the catch and taken cards (headline RELIC! → FLAWLESS! → GIANT! → NEW RECORD! → NEW FIND! → FOUND!, rarity and variant badges, weight, hardness, level); a full sack holds the find in `S.mpending` and the inventory's DROP buttons make room. Minerals have their own index section and their own rarity (`mineralRarityOf`: relic for the once-ever ones; the rare flag is epic at hardness 8+ or mine weight 3 or under; otherwise 22+ common, 12+ uncommon, 7+ rare, under 7 epic).
- **Mining gear is ladders too, and each does one thing** (mirroring the other two). The **pick** (`PICKS`, eight rungs: stone maul, copper, bronze, iron, steel, tungsten, carbide, star-iron) sets the hardest stone you can pry out (`hard`, on the Mohs scale: a mineral harder than your pick shows itself in the wall with a red lock and stays there, the way a heavy fish never bites a light line; the maul takes 3, the star-iron takes diamond). The **lamp** (`LAMPS`, six: tallow candle 18 oil to star lamp 90) is how long a descent lasts: every strike, pry, ladder down and climb back is paid in oil, so the lamp is also how deep you can go (a candle reaches level 3 with strikes to spare, the star lamp level 8). The **sack** (`SACKS`, five, 8 to 100; `sackCap()`). **Charges** (`CHARGES`, spent per blast: blasting powder opens a 3×3 block, dynamite 5×5, armed with a Set a blast button and spent on the next strike; the roof feels every tile) and **aids** (`AIDS`, one spent per descent: canary, dowsing twig, pit props). Everything bought is kept (`picksOwned`, `lampsOwned`, `sacksOwned`) and swapped on the **mining kit sheet** (`sheetKind === 'minekit'`, from the mine's Kit tab or its CHANGE KIT button), every entry with a pixel icon (`pick`, `lamp`, `sack`, `keg`, `bird`, `twig`, `timber` masks). Shops stock the next rung from the island tier in its `tier` field, and charges and aids by the pack. Every mine has a stone the maul can pry at level 1 and a direct pool at levels 1 to 3 (checked headlessly; the emitter refuses a roster without them). Saves are `v: 14`; a v13 save gets the maul, the candle, the ore sack and an empty mineral record.
- **Every island has a mine, and one relic in it, taken once ever.** A hundred and fifty-eight relics (`rel_<island>` in `MINERALS`, `relic: true`, lore in `RELIC_LORE`), each a sliver of its mine's weight (0.4), lying in the depth band its island's tier earns (tier 0–1 from level 3, 2 from 4, 3 from 5, 4 from 6, 5 from 7, 6 at the bottom) and just harder than the pick the tier hands you (tier 0 hardness 4, 1 6, 2 7, 3 8, 4 9, 5 and 6 10: star-iron only), worth about $1,200 at the shoal to $80,000 at tier 6. They have their own tier, **Relic**, in mint (`RARITY.relic`, `#5ee8c0`) beside the fish legends' gold and the creatures' ember; once in `S.mindex` the relic leaves the pool. The Drowned Compass under the shoal, the Harbourmaster's Seal, the Captain's Skull at the cove, the Rime Heart in the fjord, the Star That Fell, the Anchor at the iron island. Minerals are drawn like the beasts: `MINERAL_SIL` holds twenty-five hand-drawn silhouettes (nugget, lump, shard, cluster, cube, geode, slab, ammonite, trilobite, tooth, bone, shell, egg, vein, star, ring, skull, disc, leaf, fish, idol, ingot, key, bead; `#` stone, `r` the grey rock matrix an ore sits in, `f` a lit face, `d` a groove, `a` a crystal lining or metal flecks, `h` a hollow, `m` a fossil's imprint), `shadeMineral` lights them from the upper right in five tones and lays the pattern (facets on crystals, flecks on ores, veins, bands, spots, ridges, grain, a glow), `mineralPal` derives the tones from `col.base` (with `light`, `dark`, `acc`, `mark`, `glow` overrides), `mineralC` caches canvases. Every mineral is one entry in `MINERALS` with a `loc` map, so a real species that lives on several islands is one entry with several homes (quartz, calcite, galena, piece of eight); the catalogue of real minerals and the per-island rosters live in the scratchpad emitter and the emitted entries are the source of truth afterwards. The gallery scene (`drawMine`) is the mine's rock in three tones with a decoration per kind (`mineDeco`: flint nodules in chalk, layers, icicles, coal seams, glowing cracks, bone ribs, crystal shards, cogs, stars, roots, glowshrooms…), timber props, rails, the ore cart with your finds in it, the face wall at the right glinting with whatever stands open, the miner at 2× with a lantern in one hand and the pick raised or struck in the other (`minerC`, `drawMiner`), and the lamp's halo fading as the oil goes. The shaft head on the strip (`drawShaftHead`) is a mound of the mine's rock with a timbered adit, a lamp, rails and a loaded cart; `drawMineDecor` adds spoil heaps and a sign.
- **Own art, higher resolution than Stardew.** The bar-and-fish fight mechanic is fair game (mechanics are not protected); the art, sounds, names and UI are ours. Nothing is copied from Stardew Valley or One Cast. Everything in the prototype is placeholder code-drawn pixel art at 192×108 scene resolution; the resolution decision for real art is still open (see below).
- **Space is the endgame and the pitch line, not a reskin.** Space fishing only earns its place if the mechanics change (gravity throws off the net, alien behaviors). Build hours 1–20 first; space is an expansion.
- **The HUD says what an island is and which one it is, not its name.** Every island has a `title` ("Deserted Island", "Harbor Town", "Volcano Island"…, `locTitle`) that heads the fishing HUD, the strip and the hunt, and islands are numbered in the order you first land on them (`S.order`, `islandNo`, `islandLabel`): the second HUD line reads *Island 1 · The Wreck · 12:40 · Day*, the strip's vibe line starts *Island 3 · Brine Cay.*, the hunt's sub line *Island 3 · Salt Cay*. The placeholder names stay on the chart and in the strip line. Saves are `v: 12`; a v11 save numbers what it has visited, shoal first.
- **The world is fictional, the fish are real.** Island names are placeholders until the world is named. Every species outside space is a real fish, placed by biome (warm shallows, temperate pier, kelp forest, deep sea, cold fjord), with its real adult size range. Space gets invented species later.
- **One legend per location.** Each island has an invented, named legendary fish (Wreckwraith, The Harbormaster, The Saltwidow, Verdigris, The Jade Lantern, Nightmaw, Monsoon Mother, Rimeheart, The Bellringer, Caldera, The Unseen, The Deep Choir, Galvanis) with a line of lore. Rod only, squid, a narrow time window, a sliver of pool weight (0.4 against commons at 20–40), difficulty 0.97+, and it can be landed once ever: once it is in the index it leaves the pool. Losing the fight leaves it out there.
- **Rarity is shown, in colour.** Seven tiers, each with a colour: Common silver, Uncommon green, Rare blue, Epic purple, Legendary gold, Fabled ember, Relic mint (`RARITY`). On the catch and taken cards it is a **badge** under the name (`.catch .rar .badge`: text, border and a tinted background all in the tier's colour, via `color-mix` with a plain dark fallback), with the variant as a second badge beside it, so even Common reads as a rarity and not as body text. It is derived from the data by `rarityOf`, not hand-set: a legend is legendary; the `rare` flag is rare, or epic when the fish also needs squid, fights at 0.75+, or sits at island weight 3 or under; everything else goes by island weight (16+ common, 7+ uncommon, under 7 rare). A species can pin its own with `tier`. The tier shows under the name on the catch screen and in the index and inventory lists.
- **Variants are rolled at cast time** (`VARIANTS`): Large (×2 worth, size in the top 30%, 8%), Gigantic (×5, past the species' maximum, always trophy size, drawn at 4× on the card, 1.2%), Albino (×4, bone-white palette with a red eye, 2%) and Mutated (half worth, every tone turned a third of the way round the wheel with a hot-pink pattern and a yellow eye, 3%). Large and Gigantic come only on the rod and the trophy line; set lines never land them. Not in the prologue, never on a legend. The catch object carries `v`; the card headlines GIGANTIC! or ALBINO! in the variant's colour; the index counts variants per species. A species with `mutant: true` only ever comes mutated and takes no penalty: eleven exist: four scattered over the exotic islands (Twin-maw Carp, Six-eyed Goby, Knotback Eel, Inside-out Perch) and seven more at **The Runoff** (Blighted Island, tier 6), where every fish is mutant-only and the scattered four gather too (they carry a second `loc`).
- **The world is a hundred and fifty-eight islands and a spaceport.** Fifteen to fifty islands per tier, routes crossing between them, so a tier is never a single stop. The a hundred and forty-five newer islands (eighteen in the first expansion, twenty-two in the second, seventeen in the third, twenty-five in the fourth, twenty-eight in the fifth, which grew the chart, thirty-five in the sixth, which grew it again) are generated from compact data (one block per island: scene, village, hunting ground, keeper, four spots and an open-water spot, six to eight fish, a legend, three to five creatures) and land in `LOCS`, `SPECIES`, `FISH_ART`, `GAME` and `LEGEND_LORE` as plain entries like everything else. The second expansion added the ground kinds `steppe`, `wrecks`, `reeds` and `cloud`, the props reed, windmill, hull rib, oyster rack, anchor, grave, pages and horse-post, the buildings yurt, cannery, hulk, library, chapel, whitewash dome and turf house, a `paper` shore, a `cloud` weather (a ceiling of cloud for the island that hangs from one) and the water kinds marsh, strait, crater, pond and sky. Islands with no shop have a traders' tent or your camp, and every trader is a named keeper with a face too (Hemi the Salter, Egg-wife Dora, Diver Kesh, Pilgrim Ott, Tonger Bess, Doctor Lind, Captain Rook, Old Nan), reached from the inventory sheet; Gallows Cove buys at double (`sellMult: 2`, applied to the haul and to a single fish; bounties override it). **A real species that already lives somewhere else is the same fish on a new island, never a copy**: the fourth and fifth expansions' emitters turn any fish whose name is already in `SPECIES` (or already written earlier in the same expansion) into a second `loc` on the existing entry (hake at Pot Row, porbeagle off Fogbank, mackerel at three southern islands: ninety-three of them so far) and do the same for a creature already in `GAME` (thirty-nine), so the index never lists one animal twice; the per-island minimums (six fish, three creatures) count those second homes.
- **Every keeper has a face, and it is theirs alone.** `FACES[locId]` is a portrait descriptor (skin, hair style and colour, eyes, brows, nose, mouth, beard, hat, collar, extras, and a `kind` for the ones that are not plain humans: `giant` for Brakka under his horned iron helm with glowing eye slits, `frog` for King Brek, `automaton` for Unit Seven, `paper` for the Underlibrarian, `glow` for Nix, `salt` for Salt Ama, `ink` for Scrivener Oss, `shadowed` for the Lamplighter in his hood, `flipped` for Keel Harrow who hangs upside down). `drawFace` paints a 48×48 bust from it at one pixel per pixel, lit from the upper right with the same five tones as the fish and beasts (outline, shadow, base, light, rim), over a round window in the island's colour; `faceC` caches it at 3×, `drawFaces(el)` fills `<canvas class="face">` after a sheet renders, and `keeperBox` lays out portrait, name, TALK and a parchment speech box for what they said. Nothing is drawn by hand and nothing is copied: the parts (fourteen hair styles, twenty-odd hats, collars, beards, scars, kohl, spectacles, a bird on the shoulder, a cup of tea) are composed per keeper, so each island's keeper looks like their island: a tattooed diver, a dyer in a purple turban, a steppe falconer with an eagle, a hermit in a grey headscarf, a ticket-taker with hollow eyes. **Their expressions change as you talk** (`EXPR`, `withExpr(f, expr)`): `neutral` when the sheet opens, then each TALK settles on a face that fits the line (a plain line cycles smile → laugh → sly → frown; naming an island is `surprise` with raised brows and an open mouth; the starship whisper is `sly`), and the mouth moves for a moment first (`animateTalk`: six frames at 140 ms alternating an open mouth with the expression; `faceC` caches per expression, the face canvas carries `data-expr`). Faces with fixed features keep what is theirs: a frog, an automaton, glowing or hollow eyes and a pipe change only what they can. Look at the whole set on a contact sheet (render every `FACES` entry at 3×) before adding one.
- **Sailing is a cutscene, and every landfall is the island's own.** Sail from the chart and `startSail` runs three beats on the cutscene overlay (`MODE === 'sail'`, tap to advance, SKIP to land, Space or Enter on a keyboard): *Casting off.* (the shore you were on, your boat pulling away with you seated in it, a wake behind), *N days under sail.* (a wide shot: the boat small on open water, gulls, the day turning, the island you left sinking behind and the next one rising ahead, something breaching alongside once; days from chart distance), then the new island's title (its shore mirrored onto the right, the boat easing in) while the island does what it does: `ARRIVE[locId]` names one of twenty-five arrival kinds drawn by `arriveFx`: `wave` (villagers run down and wave, in the island's colours; on rooftops at the flooded city), `birds` (the shore lifts off: gulls, gannets, flamingos, ravens, pages), `erupt` (the volcano answers with ash, lava bombs and a red sky), `lanterns` (lights come on one by one; floating lamps at the ghats, a green one at the ferry), `bell` (rings ride out over the water), `steam` (vents, a geyser), `lightning`, `aurora`, `cannon` (a salute and a near miss), `riders` (horsemen gallop the shore), `hop` (frogs), `glowfish` (lights under the water), `fog`, `gears`, `swarm` (bats, moths), `eyes` (something watches, blinking), `rocket` (the gantry lights up), `leap` (the island's fish come up to look), `dhows` (sails crossing), `spout` (a whale blows and shows its back), `whirl`, `shimmer` (the shore wobbles in the heat), `rainup`, `shooting` (stars fall toward the crater), `sandfall`. `finishSail` then lands you (visited, order, first spot, the landfall toast). A new island needs an `ARRIVE` entry; without one it gets birds.
- **The spaceport is its own island, hidden, far, and findable only by the chart.** The Gantry (`spaceport: true`, `noFish: true`, tier 6, bottom-right corner of the world, no `links`) never appears as a neighbour's `???`. Landing there goes straight to the island strip, which is one screen of concrete with the hangar and the gantry; no shore, no gate, no fish, no game. Director Hale sells exactly one thing, the Starship, and only to someone already under an ironclad. Every other shop lists the Starship as LOCKED with a hint that a dock exists somewhere. Two ways to learn of it: a keeper on a tier-5+ island, at `T_WORLD.starRumor` (7%) per TALK, lowers their voice about a ship that sails up (no location, sets `S.heardStars`); and **a mysterious chart** at `T_WORLD.chartPrice` ($150,000) that is on a tier-5+ shop's shelf at `T_WORLD.chartOdds` (12%) per island per real day (`chartInStock`, a fixed hash so it does not flicker). Buying it marks the spaceport `known`, which lights it on the chart and lets an ironclad sail there. The Stars stay locked for now; the Starship purchase is the visible endgame step.
- **Every shop has a bounty board.** `boardOf(l)` rolls 0–4 wanted posters for the island's own fish (never the legend) when the shop is first opened, each paying worth × 3–6 once (`T_BOUNTY`), and re-rolls after a real day. Posters are paper cards nailed to the board with the fish's sprite; TURN IN hands over the most valuable matching fish in the bucket. A landed fish that is wanted shows a gold WANTED pill and its Sell button becomes Bounty for the multiplied sum. Boards live in `S.bounties[locId]` and the save is `v: 9`.
- **Fish are not strictly island-exclusive.** A species can live on several islands outright (`loc` with more than one island: sardine, bream, mackerel, largemouth bass…), and any common fish can stray: on the rod or trophy line it can turn up one tier away from home, on the same side of the exotic line, and the strays together take a fixed sliver of the spot's resident weight (`STRAY.share`, 4%, split among them, so the share stays the same however many islands feed it; `straysTo`). Strays are tagged FAR FROM HOME on the card, never show in the "biting" line, and never come on a set line. Rare fish, legends and mutants do not stray.
- **Worth is deliberately unrealistic.** A 4 lb mullet is about $55. Worth comes from weight: `rate × kg^0.75`, ±15% rolled at catch time, ×1.5 at trophy size, floor $3. `rate` is per species and is not proportional to size or rarity on purpose (deep-sea oddities and a garibaldi are worth far more per kilo than a cod), so the curve bends.

## Presentation decisions (second pass)

- **The scene is the whole screen.** One fixed canvas fills the viewport; the HUD, the tide line and the control panel float over it. No screen-in-a-screen. The panel is translucent so the water shows through.
- **Logical resolution adapts to the device.** `layout()` picks an integer scale (phone: 2 → about 195×375 logical px; desktop: 3–4) so pixels stay crisp. The surface line sits at 40% of the height in portrait, 52% in landscape. Everything is positioned relative to `W`, `H`, `SURF` and `shoreW`; never hardcode pixel positions.
- **Own look, not One Cast's.** Dithered sky and sea gradients (2×2 Bayer, cached per hour), a big seated fisherman (24×30) with a wide hat and beard, a sun that rises over the water on the right and sets behind the island on the left, light shafts, a sandy seabed that darkens with depth, per-location foregrounds (beach with wreck and palm, pier with shack and lantern, rocks with kelp, your own boat at the trench, an ice shelf with aurora and snow). The map is a parchment chart with ink islands, dotted routes and a compass, not a world map.
- **The title is a wooden sign.** `WOOD_FONT` + `woodTitle` build CASTAWAY from plank blocks on the canvas (alternating plank tones, a seam along the top of each plank, grain streaks, brass nails at plank and post ends, a drop shadow) No DOM logo.
- **The title screen is a screensaver.** `titleBeat` runs a timed cycle (wait of varying length → bite → reel in → bait up → cast → settle): the float bobs and ripples, dips on the bite, the rod bends and the float comes in to the tip, the rod goes upright while a bait pixel appears on the hook, the float arcs back out and splashes. Every other cycle a small fish jumps right of the boat. Everything is a function of time, so it costs nothing and never desyncs.
- **New voyage starts with a title card, then a playable prologue, then the storm.** A card first (`MODE === 'intro'`): the empty sea at dusk, a skiff far off, the line *Somewhere in the ocean…*, about four seconds or a tap. Then the player begins in the skiff on a calm open sea (hidden location `sea`, never on the chart, its own deep seabed) with the rod only: no map, no shop, no money, one line card. **The prologue is the tutorial**: `tutorialText` turns the tide line into a brass instruction box that answers "what does the game want right now" from state (cast → watch the float → reel on the hit → hold to lift the net → keep or release → one more), the Cast button pulses when it is the player's move, and `rollCatch` forces the first fish to be an easy one (difficulty ≤ 0.2, the second ≤ 0.32) so the first fight teaches. **The prologue's fights are easy but not quick**: the bar starts near empty (`T_FIGHT.prologueStart`), fills at a third of the normal rate (`prologueGain`) and drains at half (`prologueDrain`) but never below a sliver (`prologueFloor`), so a first fish takes eight to ten seconds for a steady hand, a fumbler lands it in eleven to eighteen, and the tutorial fish cannot be lost (sim: 100% at 250 ms, 98–100% for a fumbler before the floor; in-game a player who never follows the shadow still lands both, in 10 and 25 s). The real cast/bite/fight loop runs. When the second fish is kept or released, "The light is going." and the cutscene starts. Six beats, each tappable, skippable, captions kept to a few words and left unexplained: *No warning.* (storm) → *He did not let go.* (drift) → *Morning. Somewhere.* (washed up, `beachScene` mode `lie`) → *Nobody is coming.* (he sits up where he lay, the rod still on the sand behind him: `up`) → *Sea, every way he looked.* (on his feet, facing the water: `stand`) → *He stuck to what he knew.* (on the plank with the rod: `fish`, which is the game's own first frame, so the cut into play is seamless). The narration stays third person, past tense, and never explains. **The storm asks one question.** Partway into the first beat a brass box asks *Hold on to your rod?* with Hold on / Let go; the beat waits for the answer (tapping elsewhere does nothing, SKIP counts as holding), and the second caption answers it: *He did not let go.* or *He let go.* The answer is kept in `S.heldRod`. **Let go and you drown.** The cutscene branches (`cutBeats()` picks `CUT_DROWN` over `CUT`): *He let go.* (he sinks through dark water, bubbles rising, the rod drifting off alone: `drawSink`) → *The sea kept him.* (a calm morning, only his hat on the swell: `drawKept`, the `hat` sprite) → a black card, *Drowned.*, with the hint *tap to start again*. `endCut` then wipes the save (`freshState`) and shows the title with no Continue and the line *He let go of his rod, and he paid the price.* under NEW VOYAGE, so the player starts the whole prologue again. There is no no-rod start any more (the whittle path is gone; an old save that had let go gets its rod back on load). Hold on and nothing changes. When it ends the save resets to the shoal with no boat, empty bucket and one line, the rod; the index keeps the two prologue fish, so the story is in the record. Closing the game mid-storm resumes the storm. Continue skips all of it. The menu replays the cutscene only.
- **The first island after the shoal teaches the village and the wild, once.** `S.tut` is a stage machine (`exit → village → talk → sell → buy → gate → hunt → mine → done`) started by `finishSail` on the first landfall anywhere but the shoal (`tutStart`, `S.tutIsland`), and ended early if the player sails away. On the shore the green **EXIT** glows (`.exit.pulse`, `@keyframes beaconG`) and the tide line says to go ashore; on the strip the hint box turns brass and walks the player ◀ to the village (`tutStripText`; the gate is left, the village middle, the shore right); pressing the door opens the shop with a brass box at the top (`tutShopText`): TALK first, then **Sell the haul** (the shop counter now always has a sell button; the traders' tent uses the inventory sheet's), then buy something if anything is affordable (closing the shop without buying moves on too, so nobody is trapped); then the strip walks the player ◀ to the gate and the hunt's note line teaches by phase (`tutHuntText`: wait for a sign, keep still, hold DRAW and let go inside the band, creep for a wider band); the first stalk's end, taken or missed, sends the player on ◀ to the shaft head (`tutFinishHunt`), where the mine's note line teaches by state (`tutMineText`: strike a tile, read the glints, pry the opened stone, hold PRY and let go in the band) and the first find or the climb out finishes the whole lesson with a toast (`tutFinishMine`); an island with no mine ends the lesson after the stalk. A sells-only first island skips the buy step; an island with nothing goes straight to the gate. `tutHere`/`tutAt`/`tutSet` are the helpers.
- **Captions crossfade** (`setCap` swaps the text only after the fade-out) and do not use `text-wrap: balance`, which drew ghost glyphs on iOS Safari. iOS can also leave a ghost of the previous caption at its old position once the choice box moves it; the caption is its own compositing layer (`will-change`, `translateZ(0)`) and `setCap` flips `display` off and on at the swap to force a repaint.
- **Every voyage has an eight-digit code, and the code is the save.** Before a run starts the title asks you to choose a code (`codeBox('new')`: type eight digits, tap Random, or tap one of **three suggested codes that are easy to remember and confirmed free** (`memorableCodes` ranks a straight run, a block said twice or mirrored, a year twice, round thousands, shuffled within each tier; `suggestCodes` skips any living voyage on this device or in the cloud, at most fourteen cloud checks); the code box is on the title screen and takes Enter and Escape on a keyboard). `SYNC.testCodes` (`00000000`) are the designer's test voyages: a new voyage on one always restarts it, cloud copy included, with no drowning toast. A code with a **living voyage** under it anywhere (a save on this device or in the cloud with `started` and not `drowned`; `isLive`, `cloudLive` reads the `started` leaf) is refused, so nobody can start a new run on someone's code. **A drowned voyage frees its code**: the dead save is written with `started: false, drowned: true`, Enter a code says *That voyage ended… the code is free again*, the code box offers it among the free ones, and a new voyage on it keeps the old `created` stamp (`cloudCreated`) so the database rule lets the new run overwrite the dead one; a toast says the last voyager with the code drowned. **Enter a code** on any device opens that voyage where it was last saved (`fetchSave` takes the newer of the device's copy and the cloud's). Continue shows the current code; the Menu sheet shows it with the cloud state; a save from before codes is asked to claim one on Continue (`'claim'`). Starting a new voyage no longer wipes anything: the old voyage stays under its own code (so the second-tap wipe protection is gone). Storage: the current save still lives under `KEY`, plus a copy per code (`castaway-save-<code>`), `castaway-code` for the current one; every save stamps `S.saved`, a new run stamps `S.created`. **The cloud is a Firebase Realtime Database reached over plain REST** (`SYNC.url`, the project `castaway-976ff`, owned by the designer's Google account; `/saves/<code>.json`; `cloudGet`/`cloudHas`/`cloudPut`, a debounced `cloudPush` after every save and a `keepalive` `cloudFlush` on hide and pagehide; on boot a newer cloud copy replaces the local one while the title is still up; last write wins). With `SYNC.url` empty the whole system still works on one device and the code screens say so. Tests point `SYNC.url` at a mock through `localStorage['castaway-sync-url']` (`mockdb.js` in the scratchpad speaks the four REST calls). Database rules to paste in Firebase: `{"rules":{"saves":{"$code":{".read":true,".write":"!data.exists() || newData.child('created').val() == data.child('created').val()",".validate":"$code.matches(/^[0-9]{8}$/) && newData.hasChildren(['code','created','saved'])"}}}}` (a code can be created once; only a run carrying the same `created` stamp can overwrite it, which is the run itself or the new run that takes over a drowned code; nothing but eight-digit codes). The mock enforces the same rule.

## Catch screen and control panel (third pass)

- **Every landing opens a catch screen.** Headline by priority (Legendary! → Gigantic! → Albino! → Trophy! → New record! → New species! → Landed!), a rarity line under the name in the tier's colour (with the variant and its worth multiplier beside it), tag pills for the rest, the fish lit in a dark panel, name, size, a sub line (rig, time since the line went out), and three stat tiles: worth, bait, time of day. Driftwood and junk get the same screen with a single button. The size line shows length and weight; weight comes from `kgOf` (a condition factor per body shape in `K_SHAPE`, overridable per species with `k`, times length cubed, with ±8% jitter rolled at catch time and stored as `kg`). The index keeps `bestKg` beside `best`; the inventory lists both.
- **The fish is held until you decide.** At a market: **Sell** (that one fish, money now) or **Keep** (bucket, sell the haul later). Elsewhere: **Keep** or **Release**. **Bucket full: the inventory opens on its own over the card** (the sheet stacks above the card, `z-index` 21; once per landed fish, `roomAskedFor`) with a red line naming the fish that is waiting and the bucket listed cheapest first (only then; the plain inventory keeps catch order); **Let go** on any fish frees a slot and hands you straight back to the card with Keep lit (`releaseFish`), as does Sell the haul at a market (`sellHaul`); close the sheet instead and the card offers Release, with a **Make room** button that reopens the inventory. Tapping outside the card keeps the fish (or releases it if the bucket is full). The pending catch lives in `S.pending` and is saved, so a reload reopens the screen. The index is updated at landing time, so a released fish still counts as caught.
- **The panel is the always-there tab.** Tide line; `LINES` header with a live "n lines ready" / "n on!" count; four line cards (a locked card says "shop", never a price: lines are bought only at a shop counter; a card with a fish on it, rod HIT! or passive BITE!, turns red and pulses a glow); a one-line summary of the selected line's rig, bait, rod and line test with a single **CHANGE RIG** button that opens the **rig sheet** (this line's rig and bait as segmented pickers, then every rod, line and bucket you own with the one in use marked and a USE button on the rest, then lines, weapon and boat for reference), every entry with its **gear icon**; **the pickers list only what you have ever had, plus one empty slot** (a dashed box labelled *empty*) while anything is still unobtained: there are too many baits, rods and shot to list them all, so the slot stands for whatever you equip later (`S.seen` records every bait, shot, call and rig ever bought, derived from what a save holds on load; the rod, set line, scraps, stones and no-call are always known, and the notes under the pickers hint without naming; `icon(key, true)` still draws a dark silhouette if one is ever wanted); nothing unfolds inline any more; the rig note; the big Cast / Set the line / Pull in / Reel in / Haul in button (dark with a brass border, `.cta.go`, so it sits with the panel instead of shouting; only Reel in! on a hit is loud); then a bottom bar with **Inventory** (bucket count and value) and, at a market, **Sell haul**. The driftwood row stays above the bar on the shoal before the raft.
- **Inventory sheet.** Bucket list with sprites, size, trophy star, value and a Let go button per fish; whole-bucket total and Sell the haul at a market; bait counts; gear (rod, line, bucket, boat, weapon, lines, trophy rig, driftwood), each with its icon.
- **Every piece of gear has a pixel icon.** `ICON_MASKS` are 20×20 letter masks (a main, b dark, c accent, d highlight, e extra): a rod, a line spool, five buckets, three baits, the set-line stake and the trophy buoy, the weapons (sling, bow, crossbow, spear, blowgun, gun, throwing stick), shot, a call, a cloak, boots, a pack, a boat. `ICON_PAL` maps an item key (`rod:cane`, `line:l80`, `pail:chest`, `bait:squid`, `rig:set`, `weapon:bow`, `boat:skiff`…) to a mask and its colours, so the five rods share one drawing with five palettes and the eight lines are one spool with eight line colours. `iconC` caches a canvas per key and scale, `icon(key)` emits the `<canvas class="icon">`, `drawIcons(el)` fills them after a sheet renders. They show in the rig sheet (pickers and gear rows), the shop (every item) and the inventory's gear list, so a thing looks the same wherever it appears.
- **Every species has its own sprite, built from a body plan.** `FISH_ART` gives each species a plan (`gen`: size, body depth, where the body is deepest, peduncle and snout thickness, tail type `fork`/`lunate`/`hetero`/`round`/`trunc`/`none`, dorsal and anal fins by type and span, adipose fin, pelvic fin, barbels, a beak, an underbite, open `jaws` with teeth, an angler's lure, a tail light) or a hand-drawn silhouette (`sil`, used for the sunfish), plus a palette (`col`: a saturated body colour, a bright belly, fin, mark, glow) and a pattern (`pat`: bars, saddles, stripes, lateral line, spots, scales, lights, bands, scutes…). `genSil` rasterises the silhouette on a padded canvas and trims it; `shadeSil` adds the outline, sheen, back/mid/belly tones, fin rays, the eye and the pattern. `fishPal` derives the shades the way hand-pixelled fish are coloured: shadows darker and shifted toward blue, the highlight lighter and warmer, the outline a deep tint of the body rather than black. Composition follows the pixel-fish idiom without copying any sprite: chunky proportions (bodies compressed toward about 2:1, deep-bodied fish nearly round), thick fins and tails, a big white eye (3×3 on deep bodies) with a glint on the head, and five tones of volume from back to belly: a bright rim under the outline, a dark band, body colour, a lit lower band, then the belly. Patterns stay off the rim so the volume reads. That five-step gradient is what makes the sprites look rounded rather than flat. Sprites run 24–54 px wide and 11–28 tall, two to four times the pixels of a 16 px fish, in line with the "higher resolution than Stardew" decision. They appear on the catch screen (always 3×, so size differences show), in the index (unknown species as a dark silhouette), in the inventory and in the fight. The swimming fish in the scene stay one-colour silhouettes on purpose: they are far off and under murky water. The fisherman and the scenes have not had their real art pass yet.

Sailing lands you on the fishing shore (or the spaceport's apron). Passive lines keep resolving in every mode but title and cut.

Line state machine: `idle → waiting (rod: 6–16 s, 3–7 s in the prologue) → bite (rod, 4–10 s window by difficulty) → fight → idle` or `idle → waiting → hooked (set/trophy, waits forever) → haul or fight → idle`. The hit window stops counting once the fight starts (the line stays in `bite` while `F` is live, and `tick` leaves it alone), and Reel in / Haul in do nothing during a fight. One rod can be active at a time; set lines can all be out at once.

Catch value: `valueOf` = `rate × kg^0.75 × (1 ± 0.15)`, ×1.5 at trophy size (sizeFraction ≥ 0.85), floor $3. Weight comes from `kgOf`. Trophy line skews size up (`u^0.45`), set line skews down and caps at 0.8 on a quick hook, rod is linear; a long soak lifts either passive line toward the top of the range.

## How to run and test

- **It is a website, for phone and computer.** https://zik3434.github.io/Castaway/ is the game; `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes the repository root to GitHub Pages on every push to `main` (and to the working branch), with nothing built. `manifest.webmanifest` and the `icons/` folder make it installable to a phone's home screen (full screen, own icon), and `sw.js` is a small service worker: network first for the page with `cache: 'no-cache'` (GitHub Pages sends a ten-minute `max-age`, and without it the browser's HTTP cache hid a fresh push) so a push lands on the next load, cache first for icons and fonts, so the game opens with no signal; it is registered with `updateViaCache: 'none'`. It registers only over http(s), never from `file://`. Bump `VERSION` in `sw.js` when the cached shell list changes. The desktop gets keyboard play: Space or Enter presses the selected line's big button (cast, reel, haul, pull in), Space holds the net in the fight, Escape closes a sheet or the catch card, and the arrow keys walk the island strip. The layout is the same on both: the scene fills the window at an integer scale and the panel stays 520 px wide in the middle.
- Open `index.html` in any browser, or serve the folder with any static server. Fonts load from Google Fonts; everything else is inline. Works offline except the fonts (monospace fallback) on `file://`, and fully offline once the service worker has run.
- Reset a save: there is no wipe any more; start a new voyage under a new code (the old one stays under its code). In the browser console `localStorage.clear()` and reload forgets every code on that device (the cloud keeps them).
- Cloud saves: set `SYNC.url` to the Firebase Realtime Database URL and paste the rules from the voyage-code bullet above into the database's Rules tab. To test without Firebase, run `node mockdb.js` (scratchpad) and seed `localStorage['castaway-sync-url'] = 'http://localhost:8899'` in the Playwright context; `codes.js` drives two devices through it.
- Visual check without a phone: Playwright with a 390×760 mobile viewport, screenshot the title, each cutscene beat (click `#cut` to advance) and the game. Animated buttons (`.cta.hit`) need `{ force: true }` to click in Playwright.
- To test the island loop fast, lower `T.setMean` or raise the `wood` weight in `SPECIES`.
- To check the catch tables without a browser: extract the script and run `pool()` for every location × phase × bait × rig; none should be empty (last run, with 1,100 fish across 158 fishing islands: every spot pool non-empty; no spot is empty even on 10 lb line with scraps by day; every hunting ground has sling-sized game at every phase; the spaceport is skipped). The reach check lives beside it: with the boats' reaches every island of tier t is reachable on boat t, hopping from the shoal. The mine check beside those: every island has a mine of a known kind, a maul-soft mineral at level 1, a direct pool at levels 1 to 3 and exactly one relic with lore (last run: 158 mines, 590 minerals, zero problems; the lowest maul-soft share of a level-1 pool is 14%).

## Art direction

- Side-on cross-section filling the screen: sky, surface line, underwater, with the shore on the left third and open water to the right. The fisherman sits at the water's edge on something that belongs to the location (wreck plank, pier end, rock, his own deck, ice). He is never seated on air: the data-driven shores put a crate under him on sand and salt and a cut stone block on every other sloped shore (`crateSeat`, in `drawShoreGeneric`), boats get a thwart and the raft a crate, and piers sit him on the edge. Lines go out to floats spread across the water.
- Palette: tar navy ground, brass accents, lantern light. Day cycle through dawn/day/dusk/night sky keyframes; the sun rises over the sea and sets behind the island.
- Each island needs its own shore, seabed, village building, hunting ground, fish roster, creatures, keeper lines and legend. The data-driven `scene` + `village` + `hunt` descriptors and the prop and building tables exist so that a new island is mostly data; the five oldest shores are still hand-drawn. Budget content, not code: an island is only worth adding when it feels nothing like the one before.
- Replace the string-mask sprites with real sprite sheets once the resolution is decided. Keep `image-rendering: pixelated` and integer scaling. Keep the caching pattern: anything static per hour or per location is rendered once to an offscreen canvas.

## Conventions for working in this repo

- Keep it dependency-free and single-file until the roadmap says otherwise. No frameworks, no bundler.
- Every wait, price and weight is data in the tables at the top; change numbers there, not in logic.
- Never add art, names, sounds or UI copied from existing games.
- When adding an island, prefer writing it as a compact block (scene, village, hunt, keeper, spots, fish, legend, creatures) and emitting the entries, the way the hundred and ten newer islands were made; the emitted entries are the source of truth afterwards. Every island with a keeper (shops and traders alike) gets a `FACES` entry that looks like its island and like nobody else's, every island gets an `ARRIVE` entry (what it does as you pull in), and every island gets a `mine` (a kind from `MINE_KIND`, a name, a gate line) with four to seven minerals in `MINERALS` (real ones from the catalogue through tier 4, its own invented stone beyond; at least one the maul can pry at level 1, a direct pool at levels 1 to 3, a rare or two in the deep) and a relic with a line of lore in `RELIC_LORE`. Check the chart: islands need about 17 px between centres on the 400×300 world, and the sandbar list in `worldLayer` must not sit under one; when the sea is full at that spacing, grow `MW`/`MH` (and move The Stars' glyph to the new corner) rather than crowd it.
- When adding an island: an entry in `LOCS` with links both ways, a `scene` (or a hand-drawn shore), a `village`, a `hunt`, 3–5 `spots` each with a known-for line and a roster (every island fish in at least one spot, commons in several, the legend in one), a keeper with three lines and a rumor, 5–7 fish with sprites (real for tiers 1–4, invented from tier 5), 2–4 creatures (a silhouette from `BEAST_SIL` and a palette), and a legend with lore. Then the pool check: every spot × phase × bait × rig non-empty, and every hunting ground at every phase.
- Rosters are already deep (16–24 fish per island); prefer new islands over more fish on existing ones. The generated species sit in `SPECIES` and `FISH_ART` as plain entries like every other fish; there is no generator in the repo.
- Rarity is derived, not set: a new species gets its tier from its island weight and `rare` flag unless it pins `tier`. Variant palettes are generated from the base palette (`variantPal`), so a new species needs no extra art for albino or mutated.
- When adding a mineral: a real one belongs to its island's geology (chalk and flint on a chalk island, cassiterite where there is tin, fossils in sedimentary rock, native metals and gems in the deep); give it a `loc` weight (22+ common, 12+ uncommon, 7+ rare, under 7 epic), a Mohs `hard`, a specimen `kg` range (grams for gems and nuggets), a `rate` so worth is right at the island's tier, a `depth` window (soft commons at 1–3, the precious at 4–8), a silhouette from `MINERAL_SIL` and a palette (`base` and any of `light`, `dark`, `acc`, `mark`, `glow`); the pattern defaults by kind (facets for gems and crystals, flecks for ores and metals) and can be pinned with `pat`. A real mineral that already exists gets a second `loc` on the existing entry, never a copy. Judge sprites on a contact sheet first.
- When adding a creature: pick a silhouette from `BEAST_SIL` (or draw a new one: `#` body, `e` eye, `l` legs, `d` feet, `a` accent, `w` white, `m` mark) and a palette; the shading is automatic. Exotic islands should get shapes that are theirs, not a recoloured hare.
- When adding a species: pick a real fish that belongs in that location's biome (invented only on exotic islands); add location weight, time window, real size range, value, behavior, difficulty, min bait, rare flag, scene `shape`, and a `FISH_ART` entry (a body plan and palette; copy the closest existing plan and adjust, or a hand-drawn `sil` for odd shapes). Then re-run the empty-pool check. Judge sprites on a contact sheet first: render every `fishMap` at 3× in a scratch page.
- Fish are drawn head right, tail left; the scene flips them as needed.
- Save format is versioned (`v: 14`). Bump it and write a migration in `load()` when the shape changes.
- Testing in Playwright: the game saves on `visibilitychange`, so a reload overwrites anything you put in `localStorage` from the page. Seed a save with `context.addInitScript` in a fresh context instead.
- Reference material the designer liked: One Cast (onecast.github.io) for the fast-travel, multi-line, location-and-bite-window structure; Stardew Valley for the fight mechanic. Reference only.
